Physics and Determinism

by Sardonyx » 16 Dec 2008, 22:04

Little bit tl;dr here - for those without the time, feel free to skip to the final paragraph.

I've been doing a little research on some of the different physics engines out there, and come to the (not particularly insightful) conclusion that for network play, a high level of determinism is crucial for physics simulations. Which is to say: how well it will reproduce the same exact result from the same initial state, preferably across multiple platforms. What I can't find is much information about which engines consider it a high priority.

PhysX - has lovely simulations, but the Ageia developers placed a fairly low priority on determinism - even going so far as to call it a 'crutch' that game designers should 'get over'. I do not know if nVidia has revised this attitude or not.
Bullet - has some mention of determinism on their wiki, but I was less impressed with the overall feel of the simulation - it seems somehow "looser" - gaps in the softbodies, sudden odd movements in the actors, & so on.
Havok - has many good aspects, but from what I understand, for commercial projects it is out of the price range of independent developers.
C4 1.5.x - is still a big unknown. We know Eric is very competent, and that he's coding it from scratch, but the folks on the physics-only packages are no slouches either and have had a few years to work out the kinks.
Others - Newton, ODE, PAL, and so on - are they in the running?

I suppose I'm not looking for a specific answer such as "Bullet is best for everything!" but rather some feedback about two qualities for each engine: the "tightness" of the simulation (how real it feels) and the determinism (same results from initial state every time). Since it's not likely that anyone has comprehensive knowledge on all of the systems, if you know about one and have a few comments, I'd love to hear them.

Re: Physics and Determinism

by rainchild » 16 Dec 2008, 22:59

I think it depends a lot on the type of game you're making and where you are applying the physics.

I mean, it doesn't really matter if a ragdoll falls face forward on my monitor, and flat on his back on your monitor.

For more important physics, such as "did the rocket hit you", I think this needs to be overseen by the server and a packet sent, eg "the rocket exploded at 120.34, 18.721, -41.3, and it killed bob", the client can then interpret that message as it sees fit - move the rocket into position, play the explosion physics, do the ragdoll, etc.

The physics definately won't stay in sync when you introduce a fluctuating ping into the mix, for instance maybe on your screen you see me fall off a cliff, but due to the ping time, half a second later you see that actually I stopped in time and didn't fall, so any falling physics you were doing would need to be aborted, I guess.

Re: Physics and Determinism

by Sardonyx » 16 Dec 2008, 23:54

An excellent point - many games just use their physics for fluff, and no real determination is required. If a fire is tossing out tiny embers as a particle effect, it hardly matter how they bounce differently on different systems.

But to me it does matter which way a ragdoll falls. I'd like the server to be able to send a message saying, "A ragdoll in this position at this location at this time took and impulse of this many newtons to this bone. Have fun with it." - and have all the clients get the same result. Sure, the server can send out continuous position updates (left kneecap just contacted the ground and is now bouncing this way), but wouldn't it be nicer if the physics were deterministic enough to give the same answer from the start? Then the only desync issues are from ping trouble, rather than being built into the simulation from the beginning.

I agree that the ping trouble can be significant, but doesn't it make sense to reduce the randomness of the physics outcomes to mitigate that as well? A deterministic physics system seems like it should also reduce the overall network traffic if the clients can be expected to render a fairly close approximation on their own (though the server would still be the authority of course; a hacked client would only desync faster since there shouldn't be any real game logic on there anyway).

It may well be impossible, but it sure would be nice.

Thanks for that - the information on these engines is remarkably lacking. I'm still doing research, and will use this thread as a dumping ground for what I find, in case anyone else is looking too. I fully expect C4's native physics implementation to be excellent, but in the meantime, this brings me up to speed on some of the considerations.

Here is an interesting comparison video: http://jp.youtube.com/watch?v=IhOKGBd-7iw. It is a year old, and both Bullet and PhysX have been under development during the time, but it's rare to see a direct comparison like that. On a forum where the video was featured, a proponent of Newton came out saying the results were atypical, but it is a datapoint anyway.

I actually like the idea of the Physical Abstraction Layer (PAL), but I'm not sure how much performance is suffering by having the extra layer in there. Probably not much, but at some level it makes more sense to choose the engine that best matches the design requirements and go from there. The value in PAL (that I can see) is that it allows an apples-to-apples comparison.

Determinism seems less on people's minds than speed, but there are a few links out there with hints (seeding the random number generator and keep constant timesteps, for instance). However, PhysX 3.0 may be headed toward support of it:
Good news is that we're discussing to guarantee determinism in SDK 3.0. So let's look forward to it.
(from http://developer.nvidia.com/forums/inde ... topic=2028).

So, I'm no closer to a decision, but at least I'm less ignorant in my ambivalence.
