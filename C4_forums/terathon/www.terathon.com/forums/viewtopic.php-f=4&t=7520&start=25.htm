by CptnWaffles » 15 Jun 2009, 11:40

The most probable setup I will use will be a three step process. First off, we'll check if folks are facing the right direction. Those looking in the right direction where they may see the player will then have a distance check poll if they're too far. There's an additional step I'm hoping to get added in where I can cull people based off the zone they're in since most people who can see the player will be no more than two or three zone connections away.

Because ray casting (and especially ray casting with a thick ray) tends to be unreliable for line of sight, that will not be used for now. The reason for the unreliability is that if I cast one to the chest but the player is leaning around a corner, his chest will be hidden but a render will show him leaning and thus, partially visible.

Re: AI Vision

by JasRonq » 15 Jun 2009, 20:29

First off, the AI doesn't need to check visibility for all players. You would only need to do that if the AI had to interact with all of them. But it seems reasonable to me for the AI to simply be aware of distance to players, check facing directions and cull, then start checking visibility in distance order. stop when you get a required number of matches. After all, the AI is aware of the number of players and their positions and distances so it can react properly for the potential group it is facing. it only needs visibility for certain behaviors and those behaviors are likely to be focused on the visible player and therefor checking visibility for multiple players is pointless if the AI will only focus on interaction with one.

Lets take an FPS example. We have players over head on a catwalk, on the floor level directly ahead, a couple behind, and a few behind cover in ambush. What is the behavior we are looking for here? We want the AI to react rationally to the player's right? Presuming all players are AI's enemies it will want to kill them all. So, we check facing direction and cull those behind, then send three test rays out, one to the left foot, on to the right hand, and one to the head. In most games the right hand is the weapon hand of the model. If the AI gets a connection but not to the head, test the next if you want the AI to look for potential head shots, or else have it shoot or toss a grenade based on distance at the foot of what is visible.

Point is, it doesn't need to know if the guy in the back is visible. It can find out where he is, and only needs to test visibility if the AI is going to do something that requires line of sight. In this case it wants to shoot them, it needs a target and can only shoot one target at a time so why check visibility for three, or ten, when its going to only shoot at one. It can check visibility again when it wants to change targets.
