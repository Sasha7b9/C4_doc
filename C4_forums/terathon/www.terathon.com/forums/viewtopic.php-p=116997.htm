Re: Indiegogo Campaign for Model Format and Exporters Now Li

by TheRhinoDude » 26 Apr 2013, 13:44

In line with what Boroda is saying, my first knee-jerk reaction to this news was also: "Why not FBX?" It's basically Collada without nearly as many problems. We use it a ton here. It's good. We use it to move between Max and Maya on a daily basis with no issues. If Eric wasn't going to go the C4-format route, I would absolutely suggest ignoring Collada going forward and adding FBX support. But that's like saying to the guy with the paddle: "Thank you sir, may I have another!"

With that said, I still think if Terathon is willing to put in the extra effort to build their own format, it is undoubtedly the best approach in the long run. Here's why:

Jesse made the key point above: the future. Having worked for Autodesk for 10 years, several of them on various formats, I can say with *absolute* assurance that Autodesk will always do what is best for Autodesk's bottom line, whether technically it is the right thing to do or not, and the sanctity of the format be damned. And why shouldn't they? They are a publically traded company. They don't answer to their customers. They answer to their investors. From the very top the strategic decisions regarding file formats are all about revenue, not the sanctity of the format. I have witnessed it *first hand* with both DWG and DWF.

Terathon does not answer to investors. They answer to customers. Is it more work for Eric to build his own format? Definitely. Could he author one very good FBX importer in less time than an entire new format SDK + 3 exporters. I'm sure he could. Would it support more end users from the get-go? Undoubtedly. Would that be the best strategic direction? I do not think so, at least not in the long run. Why? Control. With the proposed route, he has full and total control going forward, even if it takes more effort up front. The future is what he makes of it, and nobody else can be blamed, but more importantly nobody else can get in his way.

Now with all of that said, the BEST part of the fact that there will be an OPEN SDK is that it will now be possible for somebody to write a FBX to C4 format *converter*, which will fit nicely into any pipeline of almost all art programs other than the "big 3" officially supported, as almost all of them export FBX these days. Normally converters suck, but as Boroda pointed out, the FBX SDK is actually very good, and in my experience the format is very solid. I also expect Eric's C4 format SDK to be very good.

As such, I have high confidence that writing a nearly lossless FBX to C4 converter will be achievable with non-herculean effort. I am absolutely sure it will be better than today's Collada route. You will still be limited by how good the FBX exporter is, but most are good, as again Autodesk provides a great SDK, good documentation, and tons of sample files. To date, autodesk has done a comparatively good job revving the format compared to Collada.

If I wasn't so damned busy, I'd write an FBX to C4 converter once the new format is published. Sure enough, I said the same thing about a year ago, suggesting that I'd write a Maya and 3DS exporter for C4 if it ever got it's own format if I had the time. The good thing is now I don't need to, which is fantastic, as I'm no less busy.

I'm glad Eric is willing to put the time into doing this, as I think it is one of the top 3-4 things that is holding C4 back. Adding FBX support would in no way guarantee the problem would be fixed in the short-term, much less the long-term. An FBX importer route would work ok for some packages, and have problems with others. With going with your own route and your own officially sanctioned exporters, this way it will be fixed for at least the 3 of the most popular 3D game art packages, and you have the control you need to ensure a reliable pipeline for at least those 3 packages. Furthermore, a well debugged, documented and example-laden SDK will be available for any team to write exporters for other art packages.
