DrVonBraun:

There are no binaries for Spark because it's a Java application. If you have java installed on your system (this is trivial, since you just ask your package manager to do it), then you only need a shell script to launch a Java application. It's there, although I don't recall where it's packaged by default. I'm sorry that you missed it.

Also, there are lots better XMPP clients on unix, unless you're using some particular Spark extension. Spark is not required to connect to Openfire. (Our last game used customized Spark/Openfire as its chat network.)

Eric, I just finished reading that. I agree with everything there, too. They're all problems. But, as he points out at the bottom, the list for Windows is pretty much just as long. I mean, some of the braindead is even by design (http://msdn.microsoft.com/en-us/library/windows/desktop/ms682437(v=vs.85).aspx).

For me, it's a matter of taste. I work much, much better in Linux. I have tools and capabilities readily available that can't really be had at any price in Windows (cygwin is mediocre at best). UNIX is an operating system designed for programmers, by programmers. As a programmer, it is precisely what I want. And since about Ubuntu 10, I've been incredibly pleased at how little I have to fiddle with it.

I do pay for that in having to be choosy about my hardware, plus bugs and regressions. And choice comes with diversity, so I have to memorize about three different sets of key bindings. But, personally I think it's worth it, since I feel like half my brain is gone when working on Windows. And at least a quarter is gone when I'm working on OSX.

But, you'll note that I target all major operating systems with my own software. I'm fairly pragmatic in my recognition that, just as not everybody wants to put up with the discomfort and maintenance of a sports car, not everybody wants to put up with my operating system.
