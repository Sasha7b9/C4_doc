Re: Rally Shift Extreme now on Kickstarter

by cbken » 08 Apr 2013, 22:18

Thanks guys!

The game will be based on real rally racing so in that sense it will cover the usual driving modes. It will also have a free driving mode where you pick a map and then you can drive around freely and not be constrained to a defined racing course. A kind of explorer mode if you will.

Engine sounds and other sound effects would add to the video but that's also why the funding is needed. To pay for sound and art.

Racing games are to some extent kind of a niche market so it's hard to tell what kind of interest there is and 70k might be on the high side. On the other hand, making a quality game is not done overnight and the cost of art, music, sound fx, etc all add up to a significant amount. I hope there is enough interest for a game like this and it gets funded.

If the goal is not met then it will depend on how close it got. If it gets fairly close then running it again would make sense. If it's way off, it would be a sign that there's not enough interest for this type of game.

We'll find out

A Kickstarter is a lot of work, that is absolutely true. Sure, you can do one with very little time put into it but it will look like it also. When I look through kickstarter projects myself it does matter what it looks like. Is it very amateurish or does it have a professional look and feel to it?

Another thing that makes a difference is what goal they are trying to reach and what they say they will accomplish. Like in that indiegamer thread. If they ask for $3000 and for that will hire a team and make a game... I stop watching right there. Something like that tells me they don't know what they are doing.

It is a learning experience and even if you do everything right (not claiming I do everything right) you still can't really know. It's very much the same as when you release a game. There's really no way of telling how it will do until it's out there in the wild.

As small and unknown guys we are also at a huge disadvantage from the get go compared to the bigger and more or less well established studios. Most of the gaming press don't bother with small indies and what they do so it's very difficult to get visibility that way. If you are a bigger fish anything you do is by default interesting. The common advice is often to send PR's to every news site out there. Yes, that's still a good idea but in my experience don't expect much from it until you have become more or less well known. Wolfire has a good post on tips for indie developers that I thought were interesting.

http://blog.wolfire.com/2009/03/5-indie ... m-wolfire/

At the end of they day, you can only learn so much from reading about something. At some point you need to make the leap and do. It may succeed or it may not but either way it should be possible to learn something new from it.
