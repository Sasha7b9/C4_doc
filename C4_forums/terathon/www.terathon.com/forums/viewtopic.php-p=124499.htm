Re: How to make terrain?

by wtblife » 30 Apr 2014, 05:27

Javier Moya wrote:
I think that the way to go is to think what part of those brushes will affect the terrain, you have the brush options for that. My advice is simple: test the brushes with different options and check the results.
The options that I use the most are: plane, brush radius and brush offset. And I use carefully the smooth tool, it will give you that "like toothpaste squeezed out of a tube" look easily if you use it everywhere.

The terrains for The 31st have been created without using external terrain generators, just a simple and general b&w map (based on a previous pen and paper design), and then we went from general to specific level of detail using the current sculpting tools (2/3 passes and then we modify the terrain as we fill the level with stuff). We've used 256x256 maps for 500m2 terrains and 512x256 for 1kmx500m terrains. These values give you 1voxel/2m, roughly. Then, we use a set of instanced worlds with previously modeled boulders for the detailed part of terrains.

Some advices:

-The first thing to take into account is the resolution of your terrain. I mean, the size in meters of 1 voxel. The units of the terrain tools refer to voxels and not meters. If your terrain has a resolution of 1 voxel/1 meter, a spherical brush with a radius of 1 would have a diameter of 2 meters. If the resolution is 1 voxel/2 meters, the same brush would have a diameter of 4 meters. I had troubles with this when I started to work with terrains, I always ended up having really huge tunnels.
-The use of big radius and positive offset values will help when creating a terrain from scratch or for adding/subtracting big details, but you won't want to leave the terrain this way (unless you are creating some kind of happy-hilly-mario style world).
-Normally, you will want negative offset values. Also, take into account that an offset value of 0 means that the brush has its lower 50% inside the terrain and the upper 50% outside.
-The cylinder brush in horizontal plane is great to create flat platforms (additive brush with an offset of -100%) or to make any part of the terrain a flat platform (subtractive brush with an offset of 100%).
-The cylinder brush with camera plane is great to create soft slopes. The camera must be placed parallel to the plane of the slope.
-If you have a wall or a very pronounced slope, you can use the spherical brush in follow surface mode with a small radius and a really low offset value (like -75% and below) to add some noise to the surface. You can also do the same substracting small parts of the surface, the offset value should be 75% or more. Notice that an offset value of -100% or 100% respectively do nothing here. You can also do this to add detail to areas where the player can walk, but be careful with the offset value because a small percentage (5% or 10%) could be 1m or more.

I don't have any problems with using external tools, quite the contrary, everything that helps and doesn't eat your budget is welcome, but I fully recommend to get used to the sculpting tools. One of the big advantages of using the voxel terrain is that you can modify it whenever you want inside the editor, so you don't need to go outside, modify the terrain and then reimport it again. We still do this even in levels full of stuff.

Obviously, all of this will depend on the type of game. I guess that the use of the sculpting tools to add details in a flight simulator will have little to no use.

Hope it helps.

by cbken » 27 Jun 2014, 18:12

The problem is kind of two fold here. The blending is done per vertex so that makes it very course. Second, you can only blend between two textures per voxel tile. This put together makes it very limiting. Unless you have to have the trilinear blending on the terrain, I'd recommend a traditional blend texture. It will cause stretching on vertical surfaces but overall I'd say that is just fine in most cases.

Alternatively you could probably use paint spaces to add in some extra texturing but I think a blend texture is the best option and it's what games usually use.

There's an old thread that would help here, I think. You can see how the blend terrain works in this image:


http://www.terathon.com/forums/viewtopic.php?f=9&t=9405&p=86066#p86066

Next screenshots are from some of The 31st levels. The instance world toggle is deactivated so we can have a clear view of the terrain:

Terrain_blend_sample1.jpg

This shot shows just a path, the trail of dirt that crosses from top to bottom. You can see different textures at both sides, fallen leaves to the right and lower plants to the left. You can also see a different grass texture at the top-left part. These use the same texture channel and the grass texture that surrounds all these areas uses the other channel.

Terrain_blend_sample2.jpg

Another example with different textures.

Terrain_blend_sample3.jpg

And a third one. There's always a change of channel when you see a soft transition. Notice that you don't need to have the same texture in one of the channels. In this screenshot, the trail of small stones that crosses from top to bottom, and the brown dirt textures use the same texture channel.
The next two screenshots show the use of paint spaces with terrain:

Terrain_PaintSpc_sample1.jpg

Here you can see skeleton remains here and there. We use a 'pile of bones' texture with and without mask.

Terrain_PaintSpc_sample2.jpg

In this screenshot, the four channels of the paint space are used to give these extra details: cracks, muddy borders of the ponds and the black liquid at the top. The fourth channel is also used there but it cannot be seen in this screenshot.

In general, we are using paint spaces with terrain to give details to specific parts of the terrain that are not going to appear in other places, so it would be a waste of resource space to use a terrain texture slot for this.

This is how the terrain blend system works and this is how we are using it in The 31st. Maybe you can try to implement other method through the shader editor, but I think this would make your life more difficult and you would probably lose the triplanar projection. The current terrain blend system just requires a little extra planification on your part before you start working with it.
Btw, I think Eric mentioned in other post that he's going to think about another blend method.

Hope it helps.

I don't recall seeing this mentioned in the thread so I thought I would mention it. I've been doing some terrain work with another team, built from an initial low detail greyscale map, we use World Machine to generate a nice first pass adding erosion and stuff, and generated a higher quality heightmap. But there was a desire to real-time sculpt the terrain further, and so for that we used EarthSculptor - http://www.earthsculptor.com/ which is very simple program, but does produce decent results. Then we tiled the final product for import in the engine.

Because that game engine uses a typical height map for import and also for the main terrain layer it did not matter that there was no way to handle more interesting features like caves, and overhangs. For C4 usage I think something like this could be a good way to get a starting base to work from and add those sorts of things later, but I'm slowly learning to just do it inside C4 completely, as much as I like WM and ES, I have to admit I prefer to do it all in one place and in the engine if possible.

Figured I'd point out that workflow though. I should probably mention the unregistered version of ES is limited to a very small terrain so it can be hard to get a good sense of what it can do, but its the only real time sculpting I've found that I really liked outside of C4.
