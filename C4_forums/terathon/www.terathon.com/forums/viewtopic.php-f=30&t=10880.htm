The videos are cool, but they showcase a problem I had already detected with the first Physics demo that Eric posted: things look VERY floaty when using the official physics. I don't mean your water being to bouncy, I mean when you shoot things with the missile launcher (as far back as the original demo):

a) objects are projected linearly, with little or no spin. Many times I had cubes maintain _perfect_ orthogonality as they were being blown away from a blast;
b) objects feel incredibly weightless when coming down;

I'm sure people will jump in and say "that's because the default settings aren't quite right", "that's just a matter of tweaking". It may very well be the case... or it may not (maybe there's stuff missing from the formulae). All I'm saying is that, if nothing else, people who aren't committed to the engine will look at it using the out of the box demos and form judgment from what they see.

Re: Destructible Environment Tests in C4

by Eric Lengyel » 26 Jun 2011, 05:52

The character speeds in the demo game are unrealistically high (as in all FPS games), and this can make them seem floaty with the correct amount of gravity applied. As mentioned, the solution is usually to increase the gravity to an unrealistic level as well, and this can easily be done globally in the settings for the physics controller or locally for any rigid body controller.

Objects usually don't spin when blown away by a blast because the blast force is applied to the center of mass, and this creates no torque. We'll probably add some extra code to sample the blast force at multiple points and give objects some rotation, but this is purely game code, not something in the physics system itself.

Well... they may not in simulations but do in real life.

I don't have a degree in Physics (only 4 semesters of it in Uni, way back when Newton was alive ) but I believe that in real life, the force is applied against all the faces that face the explosion and at ever so slightly different angles across each face (because explosions tend to be radial).

So, even assuming for simplicity's sake that the object's density is uniform throughout, the force is applied across a single face and it is constant across that face (by using light analogies, a directional force rather than a point force), my guess is you'd only get no spin if:

a) The face's normal is collinear with the blast vector AND
b) The object's center of mass, when projected into the face (along said normal/blast vector), is bang in the middle of the face area;

Otherwise I'm certain you will get torque and a spin...

Re: Destructible Environment Tests in C4

by JamesMK » 26 Jun 2011, 12:00

I guess the truth is somewhere in the middle ground between spinning vs. not spinning debris. The key is most likely whether or not a particular object is temporarily "asymmetrically stuck" on something when it is hit by the shockwave. A monolithic free object with no significant friction (relative to the force of the blast) would not have any reason to start spinning. Things get very different when composite objects get torn apart into smaller pieces, and that's when you get bits tumbling around, because it is unlikely that they break free in perfect synchronicity - one end of a particular bit might be stuck to the rest of the structure for a fraction of a second longer than the other end, and that gives you a rotation right off the bat.

So, solid boxes that are simply blown away radially from the center of an explosion is one thing, whereas things that actually break apart and have some sort of overall "cohesive structure" built in via breakable joints or something like that is a completely different thing.

That said, yeah, explosions look cooler if stuff spins around like there's no tomorrow :)
