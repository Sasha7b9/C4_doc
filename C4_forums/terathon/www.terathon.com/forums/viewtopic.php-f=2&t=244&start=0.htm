C4 Engine logo contest

by Eric Lengyel » 30 May 2006, 03:53

Announcing the C4 Engine logo design competition! Crank up your favorite drawing program and create a fancy new logo for the C4 Engine and you could win one of the prizes listed below. Here are the specs:

* The logo should contain the worlds "C4 Engine" (without quotes), but no other text. Any other kind of artwork is acceptable (of course, anything filthy is an exception).

* The preferred format (but not required) is a vector-based drawing, like what you would create with Illustrator. This allows easy scaling to any size.

* If you create a raster drawing, like what you would get with Photoshop, then make the logo as large as reasonably possible so that it can be used in high-resolution print materials. We'll scale down for display on-screen.

* If applicable to your design, the logo should have a white background color. If you can also make a version with a black background color, it would be helpful to us, but this is not required. It will be easiest to change the background color if you use the transparency features of your drawing program.

Please email all submissions to logo@terathon.com. You may enter as many different logos as you like. Submissions must be received by 11:59 PM PDT (GMT - 0700) on Friday, June 9, 2006. Judging will then take place, and a winner will be announced the following week. The winner may choose any one (but only one) of the following prizes:

1) A two-user license for the C4 Engine.

2) A brand-new shrinkwrapped copy of Adobe Photoshop CS2.

3) Any two books from the following list, signed by author (me) if desired:

-- Mathematics for 3D Game Programming and Computer Graphics, 2nd ed.
-- The OpenGL Extensions Guide
-- Introduction to Game Development
-- Game Programming Gems 5
-- My next book -- not due out for at least 1.5 years

Of course, the winner will also be credited on the C4 Engine website if he/she wishes.

by ordaz » 02 Jun 2006, 22:00

Ok, here's what I've got in mind... (Hey, just sketches, WIP!)



Both are top views of this:


I've been thinking about several things:
1.- Animating it: Let's say both boxes rotate, the "C box" upwards" and the "4 box" downwards. That could take us to...
2.- Including these objects as full 3D logos, using the engine itself to show them and animating them (easy, uh? Do you think it's a good idea? They're 3608 vertices, 7184 triangles)
3.- Putting a light inside each box, like Halloween pumpkins.

On the other hand, I took into account several details. For example, the colors; if the logo is to be printed (docs, posters, banners, t-shirts, whatever), I'd rather keep a clean and easily recognizable palette. I chose black and white (granted, there are greys) because of the contrast, easy combination with any third color and printing reasons (if you've ever had to print logos, you know what I'm thinking about). And I chose red because it's dynamic, and the specular in the 3D view adds to the idea "C4 is hot!"; red for hot, active, dynamic, powerful... think about Ferraris and translate that into "the world of game engines" (uhm... Ferraris and engines, coincidence).
I also thought about the shape of the letters (I mean "C4"); putting them alone would make them look "unbalanced", but including them in a solid, deeper shape would make them look more "solid". This way, the letters won't fall, adding to the idea "C4 is rock solid!".
And regarding low-res logos, they remain easily recognizable (that's important for a product's image), even if we turn them to high-contrast greyscale (almost b&w):



If people can recognize the silhouette at a glance, and they know the product behind the logo, it is said that "the product has a strong image" (talking ad-wise; my translations may be poor, sorry).

Ah, about the font chosen for the word "engine", I wanted something somehow "natural", for C4 has been "crafted by real people" (it's not a cold, industrial product made by a faceless corporation). 3D-wise, if the logo is rendered by the engine itself, it can show that the engine handles it smoothly, even if we add more polys. Perhaps adding effects (glow, particles, whatever) would strengthen this aspect.

Well, here are my thoughts and ideas. After reading this long, please take another look at the sketches. Perhaps I'm somehow right, or dead wrong, so please tell me what you think. Again, they're just sketches!

Cheers.
