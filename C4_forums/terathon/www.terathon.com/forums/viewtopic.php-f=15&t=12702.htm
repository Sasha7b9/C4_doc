Re: Normal map rendering issue

by Eric Lengyel » 07 Mar 2013, 20:58

Thanks. Those tangents don't look right. You'll need to apply real-world UVs to your model so that the tangents flow around the surface smoothly, and you'll need to break your normals along sharp corners (which is done with smoothing groups in Max, but I don't know what they call it in Blender).

Alternatively, you can manually calculate your N dot L and N dot H terms in object space in a custom shader, but I think you would still see problems along the sharp corners due to the smoothed normals. (And this requires advanced knowledge of how the shaders work.)

Re: Normal map rendering issue

by Dalus » 19 Mar 2013, 16:33

And it's fixed!



I would have probably fixed it sooner, but the Minecraft 1.5 update came out, and... yeah...

Fixing the UVs actually wasn't too difficult. I discovered that by using the "Align [X/Y]" commands in Blender's UV editor, it messes with the direction of the tangents. However, the tangents can be easily reset by pinning the UV islands in place, then using the "Unwrap" command once more. It keeps the UV layout the same, but the tangents are restored to their original state. That was only half the issue, though.

As far as I could see, the main problem was one of the things Dr. Lengyel mentioned - smoothing groups. Or rather, lack of smoothing groups. I knew how to do them, but I didn't think it was necessary because I was using beveled edges in a lot of areas. I've seen some controversy over the different methods, and I've experimented with using both. In the screenshot above, I found a good compromise between the two. I used smoothing groups when I needed to make sure the angles of the mesh didn't negatively influence the direction of the tangents along a given surface, and beveled edges where I could let the normal map do more of the work, and give it a smoother look.

Just gotta re-bake AO and get some proper textures, and I think it'll look pretty good!
