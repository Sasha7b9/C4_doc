How to optimize outdoor scenes?

by cbken » 05 Jan 2013, 03:28

I have done some tests with outdoor scenes including lots of impostor enabled trees and other "nature" stuff. What I learned is that the rendering becomes CPU bound with the high amount of individual objects that need to be transformed and otherwise have calculations done.

So question is what optimization possibilities are built into C4 to deal with this? It's not just a matter of "render less". The amount of objects that are processed per frame would need to be brought down. I know about zones and portals but places where they can be used in outdoor scenes are less common.

Is grouping of objects purely a cosmetic thing or are groups in any way related to the culling?
If they have no effect on culling of the scene, are there ways to group objects, other than with zones, and exclude them from processing?

Visibility occluders? These have come up in posts in the past. How efficient are they to use? Are they suited for culling out smaller objects from processing or mainly for large areas?

Fog could be used for general distance culling but fog is not always desirable. The other alternative is setting the far clip plane at an appropriate distance but either way it doesn't provide much fine grained control so I'm not sure how effective it is really.

These days with a number of CPU cores available it would be good if all the processing could be spread out on threads. I don't know how much of this is already being done.

Are there other methods I haven't thought of that can be used to cut down on the processing of static objects?

Re: How to optimize outdoor scenes?

by Eric Lengyel » 05 Jan 2013, 11:49

How many objects are we talking about here? In our tests, the engine can handle quite a lot before it becomes CPU bound, but note that there's a large different between debug and optimized performance, especially when the gcc compiler is used.

The engine automatically groups nodes into a hierarchical visibility graph, and there's nothing you need to do manually. Grouping nodes in the transform tree inside the editor has no effect on this visibility graph, so there's no need to do anything there. Using occlusion portals and/or occlusion spaces will help reduce the load on the GPU, but not on the CPU.

Re: How to optimize outdoor scenes?

by Eric Lengyel » 06 Jan 2013, 18:41

From the release notes for version 2.8:

This box should be resized in worlds with large outdoor areas in the infinite zone so that the box encloses all static nodes other than terrain and water.


Make the box big enough to enclose everything except terrain and water, which have separate visibility hierarchies and typically extend far beyond the area of play anyway. The smallest box possible is the most efficient.

Re: How to optimize outdoor scenes?

by mschifter » 07 Jan 2013, 09:28

How can i use the frame time this to measure the performance?


Hi ska,

The first thing to do is move the camera around your scene and try to find a spot that seems to represent the slowest frame rate, aka worst case scene. You can use Eric's stats panel to see what's in the scene. Then run around a hundred frames and in code add up frame time for the last 100 frames then average it, or use the frame time panel that Eric wrote. As long as the camera and scene don't change significantly, the numbers you get should be stable. If the frame time jumps around, you should run long enough to get a good sample of frame times -- then average it out and perhaps also compute worst and best case frame times.

by MACK » 07 Jan 2013, 13:55

Yea statements about FPS can be a little misleading. FPS is a legitimate performance value but you have to look at it in a lot of different ways. (as already mentioned)

The thing is, telling someone you gained X FPS is a little misleading unless you also give a baseline FPS.
So, Baselines FPS 35 with a gain of 10 FPS is quite good.
Contrasted with a baseline FPS of 1000 with an FPS increase of 10 is negligible.

As a quick example of some of the flaws using FPS:
Imagine 1 second in time. And the game freezes video for 1/2 a second resulting in only 1 frame for that entire 1/2 second.
It then proceeds @ 200 FPS. It repeats this every second.
Total FPS: ~100.5 FPS
Actual playable performance: Totally horrible.

If you see a high FPS with jerky response then you know there are problems.
If you get > 40 FPS that's pretty good as far as playability BUT if your card normally gives you 200 FPS, and you drop down to 40 during certain parts of your game, then you know there is still a performance issue.

So, as long as you remember these pitfalls, and watch out for them... using FPS to measure your game's performance is ok.
But yea, it would be great to have a tool for C4 that also showed Min FPS, Avg FPS, Max FPS and you would probably want something that showed how much rendering actually occurred for a certain below acceptable cutoff... for example anything less than 30 FPS in gaming is not good. But the cutoff should be up to the developer.

There are benchmarking tools that can actually graph out your FPS.
Perhaps one day someone will make a very cool plugin for C4 for this.

@Ska,
You can still use FPS using the "rate" command as a measure of performance. Just watch out for the pitfalls.
