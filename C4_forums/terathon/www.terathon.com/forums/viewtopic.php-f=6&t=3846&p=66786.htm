I don't think you would even need to write an ogg decoder. I would look at the OpenAL/Ogg libs, they are just like using OpenGL, with all 3D stuff and streaming. However I think that C4 has already it's own 3D system and streaming for sounds with all kind of doppler effects and stuff, so it might be a decision whether to replace the C4 sound system with OpenAL/Ogg or just to convert Ogg to ADPCM 4:1. Just found this example, it looks very much like OpenGL programming:
Code: Select all
ALuint mALSource , mALBuffer ;
alGenSources( 1 , &mALSource );
alSource3f( mALSource , AL_POSITION       , 0.0f , 0.0f , 0.0f );
alSource3f( mALSource , AL_VELOCITY       , 0.0f , 0.0f , 0.0f );
alSource3f( mALSource , AL_DIRECTION      , 0.0f , 0.0f , 0.0f );
alSourcef ( mALSource, AL_ROLLOFF_FACTOR  , 0.0                );
alSourcei ( mALSource, AL_SOURCE_RELATIVE , AL_TRUE            );
 
mALBuffer = LoadSoundFromFile( "../file.ogg" );
 
alSourcei(mALSource, AL_BUFFER , mALBuffer);
alSourcei(mALSource, AL_LOOPING, AL_TRUE);
alSourcePlay( mALSource ) ;

Re: Implementing Ogg decoder

by Lumooja » 04 Dec 2007, 18:35

ADPCM is not the greatest quality, however it's fast and simple. The biggest flaw you can hear in ADPCM recordings is the background "hiss". It doesn't bring sound dynamics as good as Ogg, because it's always 4:1 (4 bits per 1 sample), while Ogg has fully dynamic compression (it uses floating point number for BPS, could be between 4.0F-16.0F, just my guess).

by Eric Lengyel » 13 Dec 2007, 14:37

The quality of ADPCM playback is really dependent on the quality of the compressor. We use it in the engine because it is extremely fast to decode. If you use something as complex as ogg, you'll be eating up precious CPU time for no good reason.

Re: Implementing Ogg decoder

by MattF » 04 Jun 2009, 01:15

cboyce wrote:
Gauntlet wrote:For indie games and such a small download size is desirable, so an ogg decoder, while CPU intense, might be a good way to hold a game small.


If your goal is a small download size, you may consider using a compressed format like ogg and then converting to a less compressed format on installation.


That would be moot anyway since I assume the game's installer or zip would be compressed, which would essentially do the exact same thing as compressing the audio into a different format. If you want to keep the installed size small, here's my suggestion:

Most of your sounds probably won't take up more than 100k. If you're approaching sizes that are way higher than that, say 10MB or more, and have quite a few sounds that do take up that much space, you could simply decompress them to ADPCM on app startup and then delete the ADPCM files on shutdown. It would mean longer startup times, but if installed size is really important to you, you may want to make that sacrifice -- that way you keep C4's audio system intact and your installed size (when the game isn't running) much smaller. A 10MB ADPCM wav would probably compress to only a few hundred kilobytes at a decent encode bitrate. Simply make a splash screen for your game, decompress the sounds during app initialization while the splash screen is rendering (you could even have a textbox or progress meter on the splash screen to indicate that sound is decompressing so the user doesn't wonder what the hell is happening) and you're set. You'd be surprised but a lot of games spend time during startup decompressing, initializing various systems, etc. And on a decent CPU, it doesn't vorbis long to decode provided you convert it to the proper format. You can use an external program call from inside the initialization function to achieve this, perhaps with a command-line app or a combination of command-line apps (although it'd be up to you to find them, I don't use vorbis although I'm pretty damn sure you can find something that does this).

Anyway, just my $0.02.

I've made some tests back in August 2008 (viewtopic.php?p=48838#p48838): "A quick test showed that my AMD Athlon 64 X2 Dual Core 3800+ (2 GHz) decodes 6 (a couple of minutes long) Ogg Vorbis music files which are played at the same time with an overall load of 5-8% (both cores) from within C4. Today I've played the whole Dark City soundtrack at the same time, decdoing and playing 14 songs at once. With optimized code, the same CPU had a load of 4-12% (mean of ~8%). Decoding and playing the soundtrack twice at once gave the CPU a load of 12-20% (mean of ~16%). Can't say much about memory consumption atm (there is a cache of 512 KB and a 64 KB decoding buffer atm per resource).

14 tracks played at the same time is probably still good music to some .
