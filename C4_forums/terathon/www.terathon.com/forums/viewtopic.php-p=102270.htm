Personally I noticed that with any game and any engine, using physics to control the main character is a bad idea (of course there are instances where I'd prefer to use physics, like for when the player is to fly or fall). Because the player will "lose" a little bit of their overall control over their character. I found it best to use just the physics engine to detect collisions and react based on those collision, but character movement is done in a very similar fashion to how it was done in C4 BEFORE physics was implement.

Doing it this way (using SetNodePosition, instead of SetExternalForce) allows the character to move past points that were previously glitching due to that bug...

TL;DR: The overall control scheme has a better "feel" if you avoid using physics for control.

by CodeNewt » 12 Oct 2011, 16:20

Eric Lengyel wrote:Using physics for the characters has a huge number of benefits that I'm sure you haven't realized. It's the right way to do it, and there would be a large number of ridiculous hacks otherwise. The issue with the seams wasn't really a bug, but a temporary limitation due to some important things not being implemented yet.


I know there are plenty of benefits for using the physics engine, but in the specific case of my game it turned out to be a more simple to use DetectCollision to detect the next location where the player can go. This allowed me to detected large cliffs (where the user should stop), small cliffs (where the user can climb down), steps/staircases/ramps (without getting caught on geometry edges and getting "stuck"), and vertical faces that the player can't pass through. All of this turned out to only take up 12 lines of code, to detect all those cases.

Basically, two DetectCollision calls. One to scan for objects from his center of mass to the destination position, one scan for objects half a meter up from his COM (so he can have a separate animation for climbing larger objects and have a separate animation for just stepping up a staircase) and a full meter down from his COM to detect steps (so simple walk animation just to walk down the steps) and anything within 0m-0.25m below him is considered a "step", anything between 0.25m-0.5m is a "drop" and he "jumps" down, and anything in between 0.75-1m is a "climb down + drop", past 1m he just stops at the edge instead.

Since the game is a 3rd person, top down perspective, I wanted to ensure the player had full control over his character.

Also, I'm not technically "disabling" any physics, I'm just using "SetRigidBodyPosition" instead of "SetExternalForce" to move him forward. Everything else still works.

But yes, I'm sure there are benefits I have not realized yet.
