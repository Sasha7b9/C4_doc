Re: Physics video of the day

by Vaiski » 16 Sep 2009, 11:47

lazarus wrote:There's nothing special in Euphoria that I can see, it's basically just basic physics/ragdoll + basic IK. Also, don't get confused with max. As I failed to explain well in some other thread, IK inside games is 100% dynamic, IK in animation tools is 100% static, and never the twain shall meet. Neither of them are anything to get excited about, either, but without some ingame IK you can't make feet stick to stairs or titled surfaces.


Well sure, feet sticking to stairs is basic IK. And that's the level of IK we see in most games today. A character reacting to falling object by first trying to cover himself with his arms and then stepping away from the danger is closer to some sort of combination of AI and IK... At least it's far from basic IK or ragdoll physics.
I'm not sure what you mean with 100% static IK animation in 3d programs. The final rendered movie frame is static (just like in real-time rendered frames) but when you're actually animating a character all IK targets are completely dynamic and can locate anywhere you want. In 3ds you can have user, script or physics simulation to decide where IK targets are located. Solvers then calculate bone rotations in real-time. Is it really that different than having game logic or game physics decide where each target is? Ofcourse when doing rendered 3d animation you can have much more complicated character setups and solvers than in games.

IK and Euphoria like technology is interesting for content creators because you can have more dynamic behavior on your game characters compared to static FK animations. Our pumpkinhead dies same way no matter if it was hit by a bullet, explosion or by falling. It also makes animators life easier because you don't have to have a separate animation for each interaction type in your game world. I personally wouldn't mind having couple of basic solver types in C4 in the future. But physics, foliage etc. are much higher priority for most of us.
