by ca$per » 21 Jun 2011, 04:17

Eric Lengyel wrote:In C4's implementation, your ships can have wakes, objects can make waves when they're dropped into the water, etc.

Just made a few experiments and it looks like for this to work visually good, it needs a very dense water mesh, simply because waves are geting too choppy and too big. And that leads to a very large number of triangles per geometry, a large number of geometries and summary on the screen and by processing there will be alot of them (no LODs, so the number of triangles won't decrease with distance). Also the water is not moving with a camera, so it's hard to make an infinite ocean surface. And because of this we have to make a very-very large water block. BTW, when a camera approaches the edge of it (where the horizon water stars) we can see texture distortions, due to long triangles. This happens if a horizon is set too large. This also might lead to jerkiness of geometry at intersection places (haven't tried it with water, but tried with simple planes).

P.S. Another note, is when changing waves speed, the elevation of the waves becomes almost out of control. At low values they start to flood the island. At higher values their maximum elevation decreases. With this it's hard to predict at what height the buildings and plants on the island should reside, because you can't just cap the waves height.

ca$per wrote:Just made a few experiments and it looks like for this to work visually good, it needs a very dense water mesh, simply because waves are geting too choppy and too big.


I don't think that is the case. You should be able to simply use less intense wave generator parameters. I recommend putting a point wave generator at the front of a ship and modifying the elevation delta value based on the speed of the ship. (The slower the ship is moving, the lower the value should be, all the way to zero for a stopped ship.)

ca$per wrote:BTW, when a camera approaches the edge of it (where the horizon water stars) we can see texture distortions, due to long triangles. This happens if a horizon is set too large.


How large do you mean? At any distance within supported hardware precision, there should be no distortion whatsoever.

ca$per wrote:P.S. Another note, is when changing waves speed, the elevation of the waves becomes almost out of control. At low values they start to flood the island. At higher values their maximum elevation decreases. With this it's hard to predict at what height the buildings and plants on the island should reside, because you can't just cap the waves height.


One thing to consider is that if all your wave generators add to the elevation of the water, then it will increase the entire water level somewhat. The island level uses subtractive wave generators in equal proportions to the additive ones to ensure that the average sea level remains the same.
