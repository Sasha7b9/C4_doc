Re: Comparison

by commador » 16 Nov 2008, 04:07

Yes there is a reason. Since every application has different quirks, it makes sense to show what a model viewer vs C4 does. Sure you could say "good enough" to throw diffuse specular and normal (plus any others) and say its close enough, but with different variables there is enough of a difference. For example, with a cubemap enabled, the specular map will still show the details of areas on the unlit side of the model that should only be visible in direct light. When using the model viewer, or even the 3d package to create the initial textures what you see is not always what you get when its finally imported. By showing this comparison it will give a better representation of what to expect when its in C4. Since the final product is being rendered by the engine, it should hopefully take some time out of the iterations by eliminating the guesswork. Ill have the C4 version of the xNormal scene done in the morning.


bryced wrote:Lack of c4 pics aside, is there a point to showing off a render of a single object and pointing out differences when the original model doesn't really have much in the way of materials that would make any comparison meaningful? The scope will be less glowy and there will be slightly fewer shadows, but aside from that nothing much should change.


Material wise, you have glass metal and plastic, each that have very different characteristics. How does plastic look in a 3d app vs C4? metal? glass? Which options needs enabled to make it look a specific way? Thats the purpose of this. By archiving what to do to achieve a look, should hopefully allow people to spend less time tweaking thier textures to get the end result they want.

by dbzprogrammer » 17 Nov 2008, 12:07

What I like about the xNormal shots, and I'm not sure what exactly's doing it, but I believe it's ambient occlusion, is the soft edges on the model. Look at the cartridges on both models when they're attached to the gun. xNormal definitely has a corner, but it's "rounded." C4, the two surfaces meet at a line, they intersect, they don't "blend" together. Simply put, the material looks more "real." The shading, however, is inaccurate in xNormal. C4 has great self shadowing and detail, and the scope simply looks properly shaded. The lighting/brightness looks realistic in C4, but the surfaces don't seem realistic.

While I hate to overuse it, I'd say Crysis does a spectacular job of putting these two together. In Crysis, the geometrical contours were not sharp. All similar surfaces appeared to be seamless. I personally believe, above adding HDR or SSS, we should find an accurate method of making contiguous geometry sharing the same material "seamless."
