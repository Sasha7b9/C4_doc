BattleForge multiplayer

by Staples » 31 May 2009, 20:08

I'm quite interesting on looking into how games do networking lately, recently I tried out Battleforge since it became free to play.

Does anyone have any thoughts on how the BattleForge multiplayer works? They are kinda calling this game a MMOG, but its a little different to what you would expect based on that.

Anyhow it is an RTS game that has co-op and pvp modes. Some of the maps allow up to 12 players at once!

Basically what happens is
- You log in, this puts you in a kind of lobby, here you can form groups/parties with people (almost like a traditional MMO).
- Then you go to create or join a game and pick the mission from the map, at which point people join your game
- Each player can have maybe 50 controllable units, not including buildings (towers) of which you seem to be able to have unlimited. So including buildings you could easily get to 100 units each, at 12 players that could be 1200 units to keep track of.

Now RTS games tend to be Peer-2-Peer, however 12 players would surely kill Peer-2-Peer with the scalability.
- Because it works very easily (it simply works without having to port forward etc) I am thinking it may be client-server. Just about any peer-2-peer game I have played online you need to forward ports to find the game, mind you there are some client-server games where this happens too.
- There are knockback effects so units get thrown in certain directions, I would assume that they are simply running each client in sync and each calculation would be done the same so units end up in the same spot after a knockback.
- I would also assume they simply track the commands issued by players, rather than the position of each unit which would be way too much data for the network.

1. Any thoughts on this?
2. Is there a good way to find out if its peer-to-peer or client-server?
3. Why do you have to port forward in some games and not others?

Thanks

Re: BattleForge multiplayer

by Eeyore » 31 May 2009, 20:13

You only really need one UDP port open to do any networking of any sort, theoretically. If they use UPNP then it doesn't matter what you have forwarded etc., it will still find a way to get out, so long as the player didn't actively block it off.

It's unlikely it's client server, and having it be client server would not help with port forwarding. It really gives zero advantage at all to use client server except for letting the company limit cheating a bit more and (mostly) to keep control of the players ala steam. If anything it will have a lot of disadvantages because the server becomes a bottleneck.

But if its peer2peer for 12 players doesn't that also become a bottleneck - I thought that was why most RTS games tend to stick to 8 players maximum? The more players you have, everything grows exponentially, so as opposed to client-server where like you say the server becomes the bottleneck, in p2p when you get to a certain amount of players the clients start to become the bottleneck.

Take demigod for example (perhaps a bad example), they are now saying if you want to play 5v5, you have to have an internet connection with a very good upload speed or you will have problems. In addition to that they also require you to have at least 20 ports open to connect properly to people (although they are setting up some proxy stuff etc).

I'm not sure if demigod was just really badly designed or what, but apparently most other p2p games have problems if you don't open ports (company of heroes etc).

I'm still learning so maybe games like demigod weren't done correctly and are just giving peer-2-peer a bad rep.
