Maya LT and Blender

by a2k » 14 Mar 2014, 15:32

I wanted to carry over discussion from the OGEX Maya plugin thread.

I've been exploring my DCC options recently, and while Blender is usable, I miss Maya. I had an old 32-bit copy. Now, Maya LT is released, and seems to be very affordable.

After having written off Blender over two decades ago, I've been dabbling back in Blender 2.6.8 and above, and it's actually shaping up to be a competent competitor to the commercial DCCs. Its modeling tools are very extensive, intuitive, and a lot faster to use once you get the hang of them, but I can't say much for spline/shape creation, animation, and, while the texturing tools are satisfactory to use, it's still confused with the older Blender material system, and getting them to work in the viewport is a slight hassle, and there's no material library. And one of the key breakers for me is that with Blender's right click selection, I can't use my tablet/stylus effectively, as I do with Maya.

It's still not at the level that Maya is at, and that concerns me. I'm an experienced Maya user, and am thinking of going the Maya LT route. It seems affordable and cost effective at a $50/month or $400/year subscription.

Just wanted to start an in-depth discussion on these DCC options, if anyone has any input.

Re: Maya LT and Blender

by datalol-jack » 15 Mar 2014, 18:51

Hmm, looking at the other thread, if the OP is considering alternatives and is happy to work with Collada, then mentioning Modo, Houdini and Softimage is worthwhile. Modo is new, cheaper to buy outright and has better modelling facilities. Houdini is in the same ballpark as a full version of Maya but has superior dynamics, post effects and compositing (mostly better procedural tools). Softimage is like an unholy cross of Max and Maya with quirky tools and a fast workflow; an Autodesk product now, cheaper and has node-based scripting and decent dynamics.

Now, since you got me interested in the topic:
http://www.autodesk.co.uk/products/maya-lt/compare

Maya LT is basically Maya with advanced animation features gutted, no plugins or scripting functionality, minus their offline renderer/dynamics/compositing (non-issue for real-time stuff and if you are mostly targeting C4 output, i.e. non-CG level stuff), and no advanced procedural materials (which could be nice if you don't have a separate app for that). More features may be enabled later, but you can never bet on this nor can you dismiss potential subscription upgrade costs in the future(Autodesk loves upgrade fees).

Even on a budget I would try to stretch to at least something like Modo / Softimage, depending on whether you are a modeller or animator primarily. As mentioned above, Blender might not be the prettiest bird in the flock, but it is full-featured too, unlike LT. Again, I am not a fan of subscriptions long term. They are designed to cost more. It's fine to try out software in full, get something for a short-term project or if you need on-demand access; yet the longer you keep the software the less of a deal it becomes, as you pass price points of packaged software of comparable quality. Tbh, unless Autodesk are throwing in MotionBuilder and Mudbox in the deal on top of LT, I wouldn't bite. I also haven't sensed many positive vibes towards it on Polycount. And believe me, when that community sees a good bargain, they stampede, haha!

In any case, shop around a bit. The big companies will always be there to take your cash.

by datalol-jack » 16 Mar 2014, 08:28

Yeah, between Maya LT and Softimage or Modo, I would pick the latter two hands-down. a2k didn't mention anything about seeking professional employment in the industry, and so it was assumed that he is shopping around for personal use. Maya/Max is taken for granted in the industry, although what you actually end up using largely depends on the size, pipeline, licensing arrangements, and flexibility of your employer. Even then, in sufficiently large teams, you will end up using a very limited subset of one particular tool; there are very few generalists that do everything from basic modelling to post-production.

SideFX has less of a barrier for learning: the free version is less crippled, and upgrading is smooth, while you also don't have to pay subscription fees to learn it. The whole argument is really whether one should jump on the cloud suite bandwagon and use the DCC software-as-a-service, or buy outright. Looking at monthly and yearly term fees is misleading for DCC tools -- this sort of software sticks around -- and if you plan to keep using one tool for more than say 2.5 years, costs start to add up, and you are still not shielded from the traditional upgrade fee later. As a less convoluted example look at what the price of an iPhone 5 ends up to be on a typical contract plan, and how quickly the carrier recoups their original loss on the price of the gadget.

Maya LT/Blender route isn't bad though.
