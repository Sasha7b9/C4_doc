Re: A few Questions before I order C4 Engine

by Grognard » 22 Apr 2012, 19:24

1. Probably about the best performance around or close to it, for most purposes.
2. You don't need a special tool because it uses C++ and debugger is built into the IDE (not case for custom languages).
3. You can download the tools, which come with the demo. Check out the wiki to see how.
4. It uses collada, which most tools can export to.
5. You'll have to learn some C++ to do advanced things, but C4 offers an API that keeps track of things like memory leaks for you, so it is really not much harder to use than Java. Just follow the patterns you see for your own stuff and it will be fine. There's also a graphical scripting thing that's good for more simple stuff, you could make like an ingame slot machine or something similar with no trouble, as some people have done.
6. Sure, of course it's allowed and there's always a few smart guys around looking for someone to work with. There's probably more programmers per artist here than with most engines.

Re: A few Questions before I order C4 Engine

by Grognard » 22 Apr 2012, 20:43

There was a license change but it got changed back to free updates.

The releases vary but it's actively worked on. There's always several pretty big releases per year at least, though. There was something like 8 last year altogether but some are more minor releases.

The community is very friendly and knowledgable, probably the best I've been a part of for any forum of any kind. There's quite a few members but not so many it becomes overwhelming, and like I said before many pro programmers which makes it easy to get good support when needed.

The support from Terathon is second to none, and you often get answers from the engine's programmer himself right away.

There's no voting about features but it seems to go by what people what first, then what 31st needs (Terathon's upcoming game project), and by providing comparable features to other engines. If you have something small it will often get done right away, though, and with bugs it's always that way - they get fixed immediately and don't linger around.

If you need something right away you can also buy support. for 1200 you can become a pro engine lincensee and you get 2 support issues, meaning you can get him to look at your code OR you can get him to add in some minor feature right away. I think that's how it works anyway, the sending code part may not be covered, may be wrong there.

I really like the Pro License.
Eric has always taken the time to answer questions usually 24 hrs or less (sometimes longer if generic questions).
If a question is too difficult or requires a lot of work to answer then it would require an "incident".
Standard forum questions don't usually reach that level. (Or if they do, then Eric let's you know).

Of course, programming with C4 does require C++ experience. You can still use the included C4 Editor to make games, but significant customization will require programming. Having the full complete source (Standard or higher license), gives you that flexibility.

Also, v2.8 is really great. If you look at the release notes, you can get a very clear idea of how often releases tend to occur.
http://www.terathon.com/wiki/index.php/Release_Notes
(Sort of have to click each one to get the timeline).

The upcoming feature list:
Other things that Terathon is currently working on: http://www.terathon.com/wiki/index.php/Official_Roadmap

C4 for the iOS platform.
New underwater rendering capabilities.
Phase 2 of our new multi-zone visibility graph system.
Lots of World Editor improvements.
Background resource loading and streaming worlds.
More ambient occlusion features.

Further Future:
Custom light shaders, independent of existing material shaders.
Custom atmosphere shaders (for fog effects, etc.)
Custom post-processing shaders.
Run-time terrain modification / destruction.
Foliage painting tools (grass, bushes, rocks, etc., mixed at specified densities and randomly placing on terrain under brush).
Multi-monitor, split-screen, and stereo rendering support.
Morph target (blend shape) support.
High-dynamic range (HDR) rendering.
Particle system editor.

And of course, that does not list Linux support which was recently announced.

I love the perpetual license.

Some people here do it all, some work in teams.

You will need a small team of artist(s), modeler(s), sound guy(s), at least 1 c++ programmer unless you can do it all.
(Or, you can possibly not have C++ programmer if you plan to do everything in the editor).

Another bonus of Pro license is that you get access to see the assets of the included demo games (as opposed to those assets being hidden). This is for learning purposes and does not include a license to use the assets in your own game.

I am also intrigued about this new "extra" for Pro-License holders.

The C4 Engine has made really great progress especially since 2.0.

Good Luck with your decision.
