Re: Another Foliage Thread

by jwatte » 05 Oct 2009, 13:24

God Rays are the integral of light across the shadow caster, times the density of particulates in the air. If you're really brave, you also want to take into effect the dimming by those particulates, but for most games I think you can ignore that.

For parallel lights, I think it may be possible to pre-calculate for each caster the integral over the entire surface for each angle crosswise to the light direction. As this is a simple 1D function, you can use a 2D texture to put shadowing ("ray") along U and angle along V. If you can then have a scene-space understanding of the thickness of particulates in various places, and an understanding of the volume where each caster casts shadows, you could get pretty nice real-time God Rays with single-pass (or perhaps dual-pass) screen fills. The two problems I immediately see are when the caster itself is in view -- you'll get the full-range sum of lighting even though you see a partial range of the caster -- and overlapping casters, where you really want subtraction, rather than addition, of the "God Rays." The first problem can probably be ignored, and maybe something smart can be found for the second.

Doing it without pre-calculation is a little harder -- or, rather, slower -- because you basically need to sum a shadow map lengthwise across how the eye vector travels through the volume. hundreds of texture samples per pixel might work fine in 800x600 on a Radeon 5970, but probably not on more normal target hardware

by MachineryFrank » 13 Jun 2010, 04:58

Nilithius wrote:
MachineryFrank wrote:That looks very good. The last shot isn't C4, is it?


No, for a shot like this I would need some depth-of-field post-processing effect. Though I even could also work with a depth map image as well. Often I produce this effect in GIMP like this:

  • I duplicate the image to another layer
  • I add the depth image to the alpha channel of the second image to create some depth-dependent transparency
  • I blur this second layer

This method allows to change blur size and blending strength. So you have some control over the blur strength.

I could imagine this works quite similar in realtime engines.
