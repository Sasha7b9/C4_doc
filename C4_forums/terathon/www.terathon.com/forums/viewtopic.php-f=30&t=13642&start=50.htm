Re: Mark of the Old Ones Demo Build

by jordanbrock » 14 May 2014, 08:52

Davaris wrote:
jordanbrock wrote:Thanks everyone


Would love to know what you did to promote your game and what you thought was most effective.



Most of it was a slow painful slog...we got a little game site press coverage here and there, we got some little shots in the arm from "shout-outs" from other kickstarter projects...

We got half our backers in the last 5 days...

Day 26: We were chosen as "project of the day" (staff pick) and put on the front page of Kickstarter for the whole day...so...what we "did" that was most effective was miraculously get chosen as project of the day...

this allowed me to write to press that hadn't covered us before and instead of being like "you've gotten 10,000 emails about games on kickstarter that you should promote, but don't want to promote because who knows if the developer can execute their vision...and here is another one for you to 'check out'", I instead could say "I have THE Project of the Day". So we got covered by Polygon on Day 27. The traffic from the Project of the Day + regular trickle + Polygon landed us on Kickstarter's "Popular" list.

Day 28 Was '48 hour reminder emails' (everyone that said "remind me about this project later" got notified the project was almost finished). This kept the backers coming in and kept us on the "Popular" List.

Day 29 and 30 were the end game...I was asking people to tell everyone they could tell, up their pledges, tweet, etc. This kept us on the "Popular list"

Saying "get chosen as project of the day" is definitely not a solution...because there are literally thousands of projects that get tagged "staff pick" and it really was a miracle we got picked to be put on the front page. But, I think what our experience does show is that it is totally about inertia/momentum (i.e. once you get on the "popular" list...you can stay on it and ride a wave). Sooooooo...I'd say the #1 most important thing is have a fan base going into the project (we had a minuscule fan base going in). So, your game should already have a fan base before you start the Kickstarter...like you already have people playing it in a beta, or you've at least shown the game at some booths at like PAX or GDC or whatever and people have seen your game. If you are a relatively unknown developer trying to just pitch an idea (no playable game demo that you've shown at conventions, no beta community)...then you are probably going to be unsuccessful. Only big time famous names are going to have successful campaigns without much to show (because they already have fan-bases from their previous games). They can say "I made Myst" or "I made MegaMan" and that is something worth talking about "hey the guy that made Myst has a project on Kickstarter", whereas no one is going to be like "hey some guy I never heard of is begging me for some money on Kickstarter for his retro game. He has never finished a game before, so I have no idea if he can execute his vision, but I'm going to talk about it anyway so this goes viral..."
