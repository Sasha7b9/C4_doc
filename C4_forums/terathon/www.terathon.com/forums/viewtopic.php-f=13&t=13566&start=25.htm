Re: C4 on the world wide web

by datalol-jack » 22 Mar 2014, 16:56

Yes and no. Workflow is a process in a complex system; perception of its intrinsic qualities and beauty isn't always synonymous with being effective or indeed making users more productive. I would say most users who want to get things done aren't really aware of it consciously. It is very important to observe people work with the gui, which I think Eric doesn't have an opportunity to do a lot of. After all, the second-hand feedback you get may not pinpoint exactly where the struggles lie. There's an art and a science to it, and arguably the science is easier and faster to get right first.

Some things can be timed and measured without reliance on taste alone, for example: number of actions to import an asset, actions-per-tool, time to locate features, hotzones (common usage patterns give you a distribution across the interface, so you can have some empirical data for what needs to be brought up and repositioned, made more accessible or removed), etc; you can eliminate technical obstacles first, and then start slicing and dicing on the more esoteric side of UX.

If you start out on layouts, form design, colour scheme, font selection, and widget tweaking without any hard data, it would be pretty hard not to mess up, and you can waste a lot of time shooting around in the dark. Pleasantness and usability should go hand-in-hand. A lot of modern design philosophies tend to sacrifice the latter. In short, forcing a user into a particular pattern without a good plan, on belief alone, may frustrate (including the designer) more than it does good.
