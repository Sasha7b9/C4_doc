by mschifter » 29 Aug 2012, 09:50

Zebeste,

a side effect is that the optics introduce a distortion where the center of the view has a higher pixel density than the outer fringes


Yep, this is a common technique used in the simulation world. The center is called the "sweet spot" and is afforded higher resolution, using whatever technique applies to the situation (optics, lower resolution/performance to the outer/side channels, etc.).

Re: This looks promising (VR-Headset)

by rustbyte » 29 Aug 2012, 12:52

regarding the current low-res setup for the rift, you should note that this is just
for the dev-kit, which is a prototype, and should not be regarded as anything else.

One reason for this was that it brings down the cost, and makes it a bit more viable for
more people to purchase the dev-kit.

The resolution will be greatly increased for the final consumer version, which thus far is
estimated to be release late next year.

Other issues are probably more prominent, like latency/responsiveness with the headtrackers/gyros.
If you move or turn your head with a certain speed, and your ingame view lags, even with a rather small amount,
that will be a major annoyance for any gamer ( and probably cause nausea ).
Though, from what i've read and heard so far, the rift has very low latency in this regard, and i can only imagine that
this will get even better as they get closer to a consumer version.

The bigger challenge in my view, is rather how we use the rift to control the games.
Since the hmd is not only for viewing the world, but also controlling an avatar within it.
Ofcourse its not really a major problem in any sence, its just that, for instance, when u play an
FPS, most of use use good'ol wasd+mouse to control view and movement.
With the hmd it's not that simple. It depends a bit on the expectations of gamers for how they want to
nagivate a world with this kind of input device.

Im not saying that this is a problem per say, but rather something that changes the rules about how the player
will interact with the world. The "easiest" game-type to use this kind of input would probably a racing/space/flight
game, or any vechile game, where the player "body" is stationary inside a cockpit, but can freely look around.

This is also one of the main reasons im getting a dev-kit, beacuse im very curious about the possiblities for this
and would love to play around with some ideas.

Re: This looks promising (VR-Headset)

by rustbyte » 01 May 2013, 03:17

Received my dev-kit 2 days ago ( finally )

Been playing around with it for a few hours, and thought i would post a small review.

The device itself is really light-weight, and is very comfortable to wear. However, there is one issue with the "nose-fitting",
or whatever you call it. There is a hole below and between the lences where your nose goes, and there is a rather sharp edge
that kind of "cuts" into the nose ridge which is not very nice This is easily fixable ofcourse, with a peice of foam or whatever.

Running the SDK Toscany demo was really mind-blowing. The sence of depth is really awesome.
Almost fell of the chair when i first tried to move around. Moving forward, while actually not moving was really weird at first ( in a good way ).

The image is very "pixel-ish" mind you, but that didn't bother me at all. This will only get better with higher-res displays for later versions.

The head-tracking was really really good. Couldn't detect any latency however hard i tried, really responsive.

Main issue currently i would say is the screen-pixel-update. When you look around there is a clear visible "motion-blur" which is kind of annoying.
This is more of an issue if you move around fast, in a typical FPS way.

Playing TF2 with the -vr option was not very fun tbh. And this really goes to show that current titles ( mainly FPS imo ) are not good candidates to
add VR support.

Main reasons for this are,
a) the worlds are not design for VR, things are to small in terms of proper scale. You really notice this when using a head-set.
This is really noticeable when you compare TF2 and the SDK demo scene. In TF2 the existing scenery and textures etc
are really to "high-fi", everything gets really fuzzy when shrinking down to the lower screen resolution.
Was really hard to tell the difference between red/blue teammembers that are a bit further off in the distance.

b) you move to damn fast. The "screen blur" gets really annoying in this case.
c) mouse+keyboard instantly feels like the wrong input for this. ( as well as a regular controller )

The main issues for me, has nothing to do with the headset, and everything to do with the lack of proper input. An FPS game well need a completely
new way to control you avatar. There are some nice devices out there that fixes a lot of this, things like Razor Hydra (haven't tried it myself),
but a lot of people still fell that this is not enough. With a headset you really get the urge to reach out and grab stuff, and you feel a bit handicapped
when you can't do that

As things stand right now, i think the best candidates for VR, are games where a player is situated inside a vehicle of some kind. Spaceshooters,
racing, mech's etc. Main reason is ofcourse that we are sitting down mostly when playing games, so playing a game where the avatar has the
same general posture as you do physically will fell alot better, since you dont have a need to turn your body all the way around etc.

To play an FPS ( or any first-person game ) with the best experience, you will need ( among other things ) a completly wireless headset.
Since this will allow you to use your body do a complete 360 turn, instead of a thumbstick/mouse to do it for your, which is very awkward.

Anyway, i'm rambling.
Summary: the device is in general pretty awesome, things need to improve, but they are still, imo, serviceable.

Planning to start a simple C4 project for it this week. I'll post back something once i have something to show.

Re: This looks promising (VR-Headset)

by dbouvier » 18 Jul 2013, 12:51

Just received mine today and it was definitely a very interesting initial run. Takes a little time to get it all setup just right for the user, but it wasn't hard to do and worked nicely out of the box with the demo. It was all very easy to understand and get going with it, nothing complicated about it at all which is nice.

I really like the immersion it can bring which is sure to improve at higher resolutions, and I found the tracking to be spot on. I've used other head tracking for years and I was surprised how nicely it worked with zero need of calibration. I quickly found the A lens giving me eye strain so I popped in the B lens and that seemed to help. I liked how it was all pretty much as expected, I moved around with typical FPS controls and I could look around with my head, it all felt very natural to me.

There are only a few things I didn't like from the initial test. The resolution is far to low considering how close your eyes are to the image, you see a lot of artifacts on small details and distant objects, but as this is a dev unit it's easy to overlook for now. There is a sense of blurring and focusing a lot of the time especially when doing little head movements. Head turning induces a sliding to that direction, I don't agree with this setup, I'd keep a complete separation between body and head. Drive with your hands and look with your head, that is actually one thing ww2 online did right with the infantry model, you always had a neck and you didn't need to sideways walk/pan to look around you just glanced left and right with your head and kept moving the direction your feet faced.

All in all its pretty slick and I think its really going to shine in the right types of games and with well setup controls. You're not going to want to be trying to do a bunch of swapping between controllers, but for something like a straight fps, or better yet a flight sim where your hands are on throttle, stick and foot pedals it really would provide a complete feeling of immersion.

The only thing that this technology is missing imho, is panoramic field of view.
