by Fratercide » 06 Feb 2015, 15:55

Hey Davaris
I'm not an expert but I think that the heat index in the room is going to remain the same. The only difference is how fast the heat it’s going to be transferred from the processor/heat sink itself.

I’m not a big fan of water cooling myself
I built my system about a year ago and used a water cooling system (Corsair Hydro H60) on my i5K processor as maybe I would over clock (I was also concerned about my using a micro case) however I never did and after a year the pump started to make a horrible grinding sound
I thought at first it was the pump and pulled it out because I was concerned about water damage to my 1.7k system
I eventually determined the noise wasn’t the pump but the fan coming into contact with the radiator.
I never did fix it as I have a Gene Maximus V board which has pretty sick fan controllers so I left it off.

Not sure if there is any science behind it but ironically I think the greatest impact on “my” heat reduction in the case was going with a larger power supply.
The load on the power supply is so low it barely spins the fan up and the supply being on top on my case separated from the main board transfers almost no heat to the unit itself.
The heat from the GPU which sits at the top is pushed out immediately and the processor sits on the bottom so it always runs pretty low.

I don’t think I will ever buy another one myself. I’ll just stick to the fans and get an aftermarket if necessary

At the end of the day I think how much power your comp consumes is going to equal how much it heats up the room (the heat has to go somewhere)

by Grognard » 06 Feb 2015, 17:29

How much heat and how much noise just depends on what components you use, but any cooler raises the room temp a little. Which also sucks here in vegas.

Some are concentrated much more on performance, and usually generate quite a bit of heat and noise. Graphics cards are the main offenders, but when you have an open case the fans kick in much less often. But I only ever get it kicking on when I play my own game continuously a long time, and I don't care then.

I leave my computer on at all times and my coolermaster fans never bother me. I have a kuhler radiator attached to the CPU. It's practically a must these days and it's not quite as quiet as the coolermaster fans but it does a great job on cooling the CPU.

I also got the 'server' HDDs last time I upgraded (the cheaper ones though) and not the performances ones. I forget the details now and don't feel like looking, but they have less vibration and therefore, less heat and noise. The old ones felt like hot potatoes any time I touched them.
