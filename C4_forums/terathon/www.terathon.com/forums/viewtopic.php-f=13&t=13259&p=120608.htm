by MACK » 19 Oct 2013, 13:27

Ok so to surmise: I made it thru 12mins of the presentation, then it started getting into blender specific workflows.

The "Task Oriented" idea is very good idea (mostly) with some caveats that it would be hard to implement (takes a lot of Dev. time) at the level he describes. I disagree with the layout he uses almost completely because its not practical.

Claim: Simple Interfaces improve usability.
opinion: (yes but for simple cases). In general, Dumbing down the UI rarely if ever makes things easier for a power user because they oversimplify and remove options that need to be visible. Think "Windows 8"... vs Windows 7. I prefer 7.

The simple FIX: Let the USER decide what he wants. Let the user save the layouts he wants for the tasks. Have some "pre-configured layouts" for commonly used tasks, and if the user wants, they can always change it.

CLAIM: "More/less" buttons are better than "Scroll bars"..
opinion: The implied logic is that scroll bars are somehow worse than "more/less" button and it is not really true. It's generally easier to "Browse" thru all the items in a scrollable list, then to have to click thru several more/less options.
When More/less buttons are overused: 2 icons: CUBE | SPHERE [more]... you then have to press the more button. Its sort of useless, and not time saving at all.
FIX: Avoid overuse of More / Less buttons. Such as when the options hidden are rarely used or when the UI is very cramped. We use min/max icons already, so its not a big deal. But replacing scrollbars with these really depends on the UI options to be presented to the user.


CLAIM: Horizontal Rows for UI elements are "better" than Vertical Columns.
opinion: Horizontal icons are NOT a fix to a clunky interface. They are good to have but they should not replace vertical columns because they sacrifice view port visibility (most monitors are wider vs height).

FIX: Use Horizontal Row with the understanding that they shrink your vertical viewport space. Let the user decide what he wants to obscure, and not obscure,and where to place the "pages" that he wants or does not want.


We have been thru all of this before with Micro$oft, and it does not work when you force simplicity.
It should be an Option that the user configures.

You do realize that we both "agree" on many things except we express ourselves differently.

I am saying: Horizontal Rows take up more space, they should be used, but care should be taken to avoid shrinking the viewport space.

And you are saying: Horizontal Rows don't have to take up more space if they are used a certain way, and you can always make the viewport "bigger"

I am a strong proponent of user configuration, and your counter is that its a big mess unless done carefully. LOL

Its just the nuance of "how much" and how its done... and user preference.

The key is doing these things in a way that improves workflow, and usability. My point as an analogy is that a mechanic has to have all of his tools readily available.
He can organize the drawers, and have lots of tool boxes, but ultimately, showing only 2 wrenches, 2 screwdrivers and a hammer to him, gives a false sense of simplicity when there are 1000 tools in the room. I am the type that likes to see the tools laid out a certain way. And other's prefer slightly different ways. So, that is why I prefer "Options"

So, at least we both agree that "having the options" is very critical.

Re: Heuristics: I just feel this is over-reaching. Its hard for software giants to do right. You can put it on a wish list, but asking for it and getting it is not going to happen.
Ultimately, what really happens is we ask people who are experienced to tell us how they want it. Then devs like you and I say how we can do it, and in between it just gets muddy. You and I, both work with the same tools too, which is a huge plus. But now that is exactly why I say it won't happen.
We are both developing on the same World Editor, yet we both have slight disagreement on how it should be done. Its not exactly rocket science to realize, that there is no one fits all.

So, that is why at least having options is so important. And why I recommend letting the user configure those options. Sure, you can also add automatic things in there. But ultimately, the user has to decide for themself what they want and do not want to have in the UI to make their workflow optimal.
