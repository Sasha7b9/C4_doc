The second beta release for version 3.5 is now available for all users. This release contains important updates for the OpenGEX format.

If you've already downloaded C4-3.5-Data.zip (or pro version) for the first beta release, then you don't need to download it again. The only change was the removal of the OpenGEX project.

Re: Version 3.5 Beta 2 Now Available

by Lance » 22 Dec 2013, 02:53

I've just finished having a look through the OpenGEX preliminary spec. Quite the refreshing read, having dealt with numerous atrociously "designed" 3D model formats over the years.

I noticed a few trivial errors on my quick read-through:

1. Page 19
  • "If the visible property is false, then the geometry should not cast shadows, if supported by the application."
    Should be:
  • "If the shadow property is false, then the geometry should not cast shadows, if supported by the application."

2. Page 48
  • "BineWeightArray" instead of "BoneWeightArray".

3. Page 61 (A very minor nit-pick... This is obviously the same on the openddl.org site.)
  • "One thing sets OpenDDL apart from other data languages is the fact that..."
    Perhaps:
  • "One thing which sets OpenDDL apart from other data languages is the fact that..."

I'm looking forward to doing away with COLLADA ASAP; especially given that the OpenGEX Max exporter is open source! I notice there isn't (as far as I could see) any morph stuff in the Max exporter so far, but I'm actively considering ditching COLLADA right now and putting some of my morph target/animation bits & pieces directly into the OpenGEX exporter.

As I've mentioned before, we're working around the lack of morph animation support in COLLADA by exporting weight information via Max helpers. In OpenGEX I presume these are (or at least definitely could be) exported as Nodes. A seemingly obvious way to provide morph animation support in OpenGEX (given the preliminary spec) is to have the exporter automate this technique, as in: Have the exporter export animated morph weights as named Nodes, with Translation or perhaps Scale transforms mapped to the weighting and associated animation(s).

by Eric Lengyel » 23 Dec 2013, 03:34

BORODA wrote:The reason I don't believe in OpenGEX is simply because it reminds me of a popular xkcd comic


We tried to support an existing standard for years, and it caused nothing but problems. Relying on Collada has cost us and our customers an untold amount of money and agony through the poor design of the format and the downright shitty implementation of the export plugins. This isn't the first time that relying on third-party technologies has come back to bite us in the ass. You have to understand that 90% of all software engineers are incompetent, and the engineers that comic is talking about are no different. But Terathon is different. OpenGEX is a much better design compared to Collada, and our export plugins are simply superior. It has already eliminated all of the problems we've had with the 3D Studio Max exporters, and it will make life a lot better for many of our customers, especially the larger studios that use the high-end modeling packages.

BORODA wrote:from the point of view of the licensee, who is not using one of the supported packages, this looks disappointing


From the point of view of someone not using Max, Maya, or Blender, it doesn't currently make any difference because you just keep using Collada. If that's a disappointment, then it's a disappointment in Collada, and you should go tell the people that made Collada that they suck. Also, we'll be implementing more export plugins in the future for more modeling programs. Max, Maya, and Blender are just the beginning.

BORODA wrote:support in the other engines (let's be realistic) most probably never gonna happen and it will be another ActorX for Unreal or DCC for CryEngine and they all lack support for most of 3d modelling packages.


I really don't care much if other engines adopt OpenGEX, but if they choose to ignore it, then they are only hurting themselves. They experience the same problems that we did with Collada and all the other exchange formats out there. OpenGEX was designed specifically for moving scenes from modeling programs to game engines, it's damn good at what it does, and our exporters are free for everyone. It would solve a lot of their support problems just like it will for us.
