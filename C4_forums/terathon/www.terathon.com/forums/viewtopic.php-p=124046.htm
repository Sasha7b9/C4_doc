MotOO System Requirements:


Dual Analogue Controller (Wired Xbox 360 controller is ideal for Windows. Playstation 3 controller is ideal for Mac and Linux)
Video Card with 512 MB RAM (Nvidia hardware starting with the GeForce 8x00 series and all AMD hardware starting with the Radeon HD 2x00 series. Also runs on Intel Ivy Bridge GPUs and later, but at lower performance).
**An Intel HD graphics cards will not work**
CPU - 3.0 GHz Dual Core processor
System RAM - 1 GB
Free Disk Space - 674 MB

Editing Graphics Settings:

Eek. Oops. Yikes. There currently is no convenient in-game menu to edit graphics/sound settings. The hacky way of editing your settings is:

On Windows: 1. Go MotOO's main menu and click on Controller Setup. 2. Change something in the Controller Setup (preferably something non-essential...like change D-pad Up to D-pad Left). Save Configuration. 3. Saving a change in the Controller Setup generates an input.cfg and engine.cfg for you. These files live at Appdata/Roaming/Mark of the Old Ones 4. Now that you have the engine.cfg you can open it and edit your graphics settings whenever you want (you can skip steps 1-3 from now on). You can also access the engine.cfg by clicking on editSettings.bat (located in MotOO_Windows64/Data).

On Mac: 1. Open MotOO. 2. Then close MotOO. Doing this generates an input.cfg and engine.cfg for you. 3. These files live at /Users/yourname/Library/Application Support/Mark of the Old Ones 4. The library is likely hidden. From the finder menu at the top, go to the Go menu, hold option, and the Library should appear.

On Linux: You can find the config files at ~/.config/C4-Engine/


Settings you Might want to Edit:
If you are looking for better performance / more FPS I suggest you make some or all of the following changes:

Change displaySamples from 4 to 1
Reduce your displayHeight and displayWidth (the default is 1920x1080)
Change textureDetailLevel from 0 to 2
Change postGlowBloom from 1 to 0

Just out of curiosity, was the demo made to respond to keyboard events?



Xbox 360 controllers are plug and play on Windows. On Windows, Playstation controllers require you to install the "MotioninJoy" driver; the driver is precarious and I wouldn't recommend it. For Mac and Linux, PlayStation controllers are best. There are also generic (Logitech, etc) controllers that work on all platforms.

The core movement mechanism is designed around the analogue sticks, so we are not using keyboard/mouse.
