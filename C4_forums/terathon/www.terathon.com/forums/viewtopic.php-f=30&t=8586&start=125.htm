Re: New Sci-Fi Textures WIP Thread

by David Vasquez » 10 Sep 2010, 22:21

Frank, thanks for helping us to test this issue.

After examining the vertex normals in sections of your models with darkened geometry, we found no errors in the exported vertex normals, nor in how C4 has imported those vertex normals.

However, we did find what was causing the dark sections to show up when your models are rendered by the C4 Engine.

In "corridor_corner01.lxo", the dark triangles in the upper section of the door mesh are caused by mirrored UVs, which cause tangents to point in opposite directions along a visible seam across coplanar polygons.

Please allow me to show some screenshots illustrating what is going on.

First, here are the normals for one of the darkened door sections, as shown in the C4 Engine model viewer. These normals match the normals as seen in modo:

SciFiNormals.jpg


And here are the polygons in question, highlighted in the modo UV editing viewport:

SciFiUVsMirrored.jpg


As shown in the above screenshot, the dark triangles are UV mapped in a mirrored fashion, such that adjacent, coplanar polygons are flipped in UV space, to re-use the same area of the image texture map.

Re: New Sci-Fi Textures WIP Thread

by Eric Lengyel » 11 Sep 2010, 02:44

MachineryFrank wrote:Thanks David. But does that mean I cannot mirror uv-polygons? This could be important when I paint only half of the texture for symmetrical objects to save texture space. This often happens on character models. It would be a strong restriction I did not see in any other engine so far.


No, it does not mean you can't mirror UVs. The problem arises because the vertices at the boundary between the right-facing texture and left-facing texture are split, but have exactly the same positions and texture coordinates, so the engine merges them into one before the tangent vectors are calculated. The best way to avoid this is not to mirror UVs along a vertex plane like this (and I don't think texture mirroring is a good practice for use in modern engines), but we can also add a preprocessing step that will identify neighboring triangles with opposite handedness and prevent vertices from being welded along the border.

Re: New Sci-Fi Textures WIP Thread

by Eric Lengyel » 11 Sep 2010, 05:01

Like I said in my last post, the best way to avoid the problem is not to mirror the UVs like this. Even if there wasn't a tangent vector issue, you will not be able to completely eliminate a seam at the boundary no matter how hard you try. It would be better to duplicate the information in the texture, and this would give you the opportunity to make each side unique (which would be better IMHO). I've noticed that you seem to be following a lot of old-school rules in your texturing and modeling, and you really shouldn't worry about things that might have been more important 7-8 years ago.

Maybe I'm understanding that wrong, and its due to the way the UV's are being engineered in this case, not the use of mirrored textures, but if its due to mirrored textures, then not good at all.

I'm not sure its an old-school rule to be efficient. I think quite the opposite.

The general assumption seems to be that texture sizes and quantities do not matter, but that's not true. It's not only about the engine, or how fast it can process larger and more textures, its about the stream too. Not all modern games are delivered on DVD's etc ... some need to be streamed over internet etc. So one needs to find ways to optimize that : reduce textures to their optimal size; re-use them; layer them; and mirror them.

Having a half texture mirrored means it downloads twice as fast. And there is a LOT that can be mirrored. So I think the use of mirrored textures are extremely important in reducing that overhead, and would like to see C4 keep strong support for their use.

I wouldn't like to see good support begin to fade for things like this just because the engine is capable of handling bigger textures easily. That would be very short-sighted.

As a model-builder, Frank is correct IMHO, and I believe standards like that are good for marketing ... he is building high-quality models, but at the same time making the effort to build them as efficiently as possible, as he realizes some customers would be streaming them over internet, etc. If his models were not efficient he would lose those customers.
