Re: Gavity gun anyone?

by aubrey.r.jones » 23 Oct 2012, 09:26

It's actually not especially hard to do a gravity gun, depending on your approach.

One way is to simply apply forces in controllers. Basically, the gravity gun becomes literally a *gravity* gun, with a force applied to the targeted object toward the gravity gun (with magnitude proportional to target mass). This works great for a more "realistic" force gun, because objects are going to continue to behave as if they have proper momentum. You might also want to add another "repulsor" force so that the objects don't collide with the player--I recommend some sort of distance squared force. You'll also need damping, otherwise objects will oscillate annoyingly.

If you want a less realistic gravity gun, you could add a custom constraint to C4 with the properties you want. I've already added a couple for my current game (one that keeps the player and other physics objects on the XZ plane; another that simulates a rope). The constraint code in C4 is extremely understandable and easily extensible. Honestly, the icon for the world editor is harder to integrate than the constraint itself.

Every gravity gun I've seen has had the effect of inhibiting world gravity. So, you want to set the gravity multiplier for the target to something approaching zero in any case.

You can also combine the two approaches, with a constraint contributing the hard limits of the target object's motion, and an external force providing the motion itself. This is usually easier than trying to integrate a motor into the constraint. You may even be able to get something decent with the built-in constraints, if you're okay with having a sequence of different phases to get your target object to the player.

I didn't mean to make it sound like writing the constraint was easy. Sorry. What I meant is that the software engineering of adding the constraint to the system is straight forward.

Anyway, "jacobian constraints" is the google term for you.

Basically, it's a vector encoding the direction of a constraint. C4 has a set of utility methods that solve for the actual impulse given the constraint jacobian. I don't think I actually understand the math, either. But, I was able to make my simple constraints by studying and modifying the existing C4 constraints.

I doubt I could write a vehicle constraint.
