by Jay » 17 Sep 2014, 17:33

There was an article in Nature indicating that artificial sweeteners my change the bacteria in your intestines and actually cause increased blood sugar. Study was mainly done in mice, but they had a small sample size, 7, of humans that saw very dramatic results also. I actually gave up "diet" sodas about 3 months ago...was drinking about 2 liters worth a day

Bit of a side note..there is a field in science called Metagenomics where you look at bacteria in the environment. One use of this..Researchers get samples from different areas and DNA sequence all of it to look for new bacteria species. Another use..Inside humans, we have normal "flora" of bacteria in and around all of our cavities that contribute to different things in us. You can DNA sequence the bacteria and look for shifts in the balance of what *should* be. Sometimes people are WAY off and it causes them to be in pretty bad shape..so they will take some gut stuff from healthy ppl and "seed" the others with it.

@Grognard,

The idea that small spikes of blood sugar are damaging is akin to saying...

Walking is harmful to your skin.

Walking is natural.
So is eating.

The body expects to see spikes in blood sugar, that is why we have insulin... as a response.

Excessive spikes are bad. Normal spikes are just normal. (btw, the definition of abnormal for blood sugar values is subject of much research and statistical analysis in the medical community, and there is a general consensus as well that most personnel follow).

Having said "that"... yea, sure, you can live your life without sugar and maybe you will have less spikes.

But, remember that carbohydrates get converted to sugar once they are digested.
Its over doing it (IMHO) to worry about normal spikes in blood sugar... but if your one of those types that worries about every little life damaging element in the universe, your probably gonna have to seek professional help for anxiety.

I don't want to downplay the long term (or even short term in extreme cases) of markedly elevated blood sugar tho.
Long term consequences of diabetes are dangerous. And many people have died or suffered from blindness, loss of their extremities etc....

But... Normal people, ie those that don't have diabetes, can tolerate a good healthy meal (over and over for the rest of their life)... and they don't suffer the high risks of diabetes at anywhere near the same statistical probability.
Only when their body loses its ability to cope (usually with excessive weight gain) do they get late onset diabetes... but its important to realize that not all people get it.
Which is why testing is recommended... to determine if a person is at risk at various points in their life.
So, if a person has gained weight, is markedly less active, or has any number of other things like family history of diabetes or sign and symptoms of diabetes, its a good idea to get tested.
In general, any one who has unusual changes in their body or strange signs or symptoms should be wondering if they might have some problem.
Its when a person suffers with those changes and they never do anything about that things can really go bad.

So... long story short, some people can get away with eating a lot of high sugar content foods... especially if they are very active.
And some people that are at not healthy due to genetics or disease, simply have to take it easier and follow more strict guidelines.
