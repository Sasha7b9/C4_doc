Re: How Do Others Solve Big World Editing?

by a2k » 01 Sep 2013, 01:27

DrVonBraun wrote: I wonder, am I missing something here or is it just that the people who edit those huge worlds are really poor SOB's that deserve our compassion for their terrible lot in life?


Most of the studios building AAA titles with vast worlds have in-house editors that use the in-game engine to do their world construction. Although it *is* a lot of work to do, it's not *as* bad as you imagine, taking off-the-shelf tools like 3DS Max or Maya and just throwing it into the engine to test.

If you were to do this with C4, you'd have to spend some time building in-game tools (not World Editor tools) to get your army of level designers to shell out content. The World Editor as it is, although functional, is not yet that well-equipped to do such massive content creation. The artist/designer workflow just isn't there.

I am working on a AAA title, and although the project isn't an MMO, or has a large open world, I am amazed everyday at the shitloads of in-house tools we use to get content created. I bet our tools and debug projects outnumber the actual in-game code, by files and lines, and number of projects, tremendously. Imagine crap loads of scaffolding to create something that's just a 10x10 foot section of the Sistine Chapel.

EDIT:
When I was on the project I've been on at work, we did have ideas of doing an MMO, so we evaluated some off-the-shelf software, one of which was called HeroBlade by Simutronics. Their system allows building vast worlds for an MMO, in a collaborative way amongst level designers, in that designers log into the system all at once, allowing them to build the world collaboratively, increasing workflow efficiency. We ultimately decided not to go this route, but I just wanted to point out how some teams may or may not do their level building.

The editor itself doesn't need seamless transitions between zones, just edit in your "interested" zone which will then serialize position data etc for the game to load. Place objects so it fits within grid boundaries, or have the ability to load neighbor zones.

But...if you have an in game streaming system already, placing objects is not much different than spawning a character and moving it about, just need to integrate the editing tools.

There are many ways to do it, it all depends what the designers/artist wants. And the army of tool programmers will do it

Re: How Do Others Solve Big World Editing?

by dbouvier » 02 Sep 2013, 14:31

I really enjoy working in a client/server real-time collaborative building environment like you find with HeroBlade or WorldForge, the biggest issue I have with HB is being locked into using their specific tool chain and the scripting environment, WF needs a much cleaner asset pipeline and some additional building features but even in its current state it can a lot of fun and very beneficial to see the results as the players would and be able get immediate feedback on it from others without needing to pack and send a file have them install and load it etc.

I think regardless of the size of the world you have to have mechanisms in place to allow filtering things for view or interaction and also determining visibility distances is a factor whether your a builder or a player; your pretty much always focused on working on a particular thing, or space and should never try handle the whole world. So whether a client or server is handling your location in the world it should only be handling that portion of it.

While one of the benefits of a client/server setup is having data and resources being pushed to the builder clients and player clients from a central resource server. I still think that initial offline editing and preparing assets like world references is needed. Bringing 3D and 2D assets into the engine, getting them setup properly visually and defining default values assigning scripts and controllers that can be tested and tweaked initially; and then pushed to the resource server and made available for online building processes and streamed to clients as a new resource.

Re: How Do Others Solve Big World Editing?

by Robert » 25 Sep 2013, 05:59

An OpenSource engine editor i used a few years ago worked with landscaping pages. At first you put in parameters about the size, lines and rows of terrain pages and height stuff. Then you had to import a terrain heightmap either for each single page one by one, or you could import a terrain heightmap that got scaled to fit the total of terrain pages, very good if you wanted the major outlines of the terrain to be pre-set. Of course that terrain needed a lot of smoothening and "de-pixeling" because the maximal useable heightmap was 4096x4096, as far as i remember (and the machine took a long time processing it onto the pages too). Since the editor there had some tools, brushes and stuff like that plus an inbuilt sun/star/clouds/sky system one could quickly produce some very interesting effects.
