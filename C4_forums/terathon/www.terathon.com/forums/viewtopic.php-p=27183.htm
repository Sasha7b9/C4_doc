In 3D computer graphics and animation, skinning can refer to the surface representation used to draw a character in skeletal animation.

So, obviously it means when you have skinned character, being attached to bones, and vertices of that character have skin weights, where weights define the influence factor how much is the vertex moved with bone. I said so simple, before.

As said wikipedia:
Rigging (Skeletal Animation) in Computer Graphics identifies the set of activities implied to set up complex controls over a three dimensional object (usually characters). The idea is similar to creating cords and constraints to control a puppet.

Rigging is much more related to animated films than a games. As soon as you start rig constraints around your skinned model this process should be rigging. That doesn't mean you can't use rigging for games, but rigging obviously helps animators to have better control over the animation process later.

Re: Customizable Characters

by jwatte » 11 Sep 2007, 16:52

"Skinning" as in "re-skin a player model" as in "paint a texture" is pretty much only used among game players, and to some extent among game modders. If you ask a professional game developer or artist what "skinning" means, they will say it's the process of deforming a mesh based on the movement of skeletal bones. The actual tieing of the mesh to the bones is usually called "weighting" (because you assign bones and weights to vertices). The act of painting textures for the mesh is usually called "texturing," whereas the act of laying out the UV coordinates is called "mapping" or "unwrapping."

While there is no hard set standard, I have come to the conclusion that these meanings of the terms is the least ambiguous and most generally used throughout various game production studios and art houses.

Re: Customizable Characters

by jwatte » 18 Sep 2007, 21:11

At the code level, we assume that all the clothing will map to the same skeleton. Then we concatenate the different vertex and index buffers when building a character. There is only one skeleton (per gender), and the clothing meshes are weighted to that skeleton. The skeleton lives separately from the mesh data (in separate files), and the animations, in turn, live separately from the skeleton. That way, there's no duplication of data.

I haven't looked at the Entity and skinning code of C4, so I don't know how easy or hard that would be to implement, but that's "how to do it" if you want to do it for real.

We have some additional gnarls in that we support mesh load-time morphing (big-chested vs flat, pot-bellied vs trim, etc), and then we also support run-time morphing (smile, wink, etc), and some of those morph targets go across piece boundaries, so we have to do a fair bit of runtime welding and averaging to make it look good and not end up with gaps. Having the artists author everything 100% correctly simply didn't work.
