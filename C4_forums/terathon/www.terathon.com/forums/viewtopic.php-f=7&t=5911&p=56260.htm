Re: Question about networking

by jwatte » 05 Jan 2009, 16:11

What an ORPG calls a "Zone" is what C4 calls "Level."
You could set up multiple C4 servers, one for each level.
You could provide a level "browser" that sends your character to the right server. That part would have to be written almost entirely custom, though, especially if you don't want to support arbitrary level teleport, but instead only trigger-based zoning.
You could register your lever "browser" service with DNS, so that clients can find it when they start up.
However, certain things that you'd expect for an ORPG, such as character stats that persist in a database and move with you between levels, aren't well fleshed out in the current C4 implementation; you'd have to add some functionality to support that as well.
However, all in all, you can do what you want with C4 and some custom development. Note that C4 runs the full simulation on the server, so there are two caveats:
* The number of players per zone is limited by server CPU capacity. You need a high-end core per zone/level to get dense player populations, and there will be an upper limit based on CPU power.
* The C4 model does not forward-extrapolate the player, so if there is a lot of latency between the player and the server, controls will appear sluggish to the player. This might be OK for an ORPG, though -- and you could hide it by implementing a click-to-move system instead of direct movement.

Aside from saying they are used in MMO's like WoW, I'm not sure what an "instance" is has really been explained in a way that people who didn't really know much about the type of game would understand. I've played about 6-7 MMOs more than I ever should have so I'll see if I can clarify. Essentially there is 1 big world split into areas that everybody populates, sometimes if an area becomes too crowded a separate copy of the zone may be created for the overflow, something like this is an instance. One of the potential problems with this is if you want to deal with people that happen to be in a different version of the area, I know Everquest 2 allowed you to transfer to an instance that a group member was in simply by selecting that option after highlighting their name and choosing to do so, you also had the option to select which instance you would like to enter if you were going to one area from another.

Other times instances are used for certain events or activities that happen and experience an incredibly high volume or demand. IE there is a quest to kill the head of some evil organization who happens to live at the back of a very dark cave at some arbitrary level, like say 5. Upon killing this guy he drops 1 token for a turn in and doesn't appear again to be killed for an hour or 2. You are in a group of 6 people so this is a huge PAIN. Then there is also the fact that level 5 is not far from level 1 and ton of people suddenly were in that level range trying to get this quest taken care of and the situation becomes such that your better off to completely avoid the task and what ever reward had interested you. If this was setup for multiple instancing than each group could essentially be in the same location, doing the same event as everybody else, but they would be in different copies of the area and wouldn't be able to interact/see each other- a nice personal area made just for YOU

Instances can also allow for a more in depth experience, if say a whole town was going to be attacked by a group and it was up to your team to defend it and save its inhabitants. If this wasn't a global type event that involved more than your rag-tag band- it would be a great inconvenience to everybody else who had to interact with people in that town if they could not because they were all in hiding for the 1 groups event.

Then of course there are "THOSE people". You only have to play 1-2 online games to run into one. You spend 30 hours working on completing something, get to the end, and then some random person 10 times your level runs in and kills what ever you needed for absolutely no gain just to piss you off. For in depth time consuming painstaking quests- something like an instance for the final battle could make sense. Or your playing an online FPS and somebody in your team kills you all and then quits... doesn't fit in with the instance issue but describes the type of individual.

A side note about having to spread a world across a number of machines- usually all of the files and textures that make up the world are client side and each person had the game run them from their own machine- its why many MMORPGS have system folders 20GB in size or more. Essentially information about relative location and stuff is sent back and forth from the server, all the visual stuff is available clientside but everything that controls how the world operates on its own for the NPCs is regulated server side. An example is you used to be able to use the Demo for Everquest1 to load levels and navigate around in them, you could see the world and all the textures (secret areas!) etc... but it was unpopulated.

To go back to the original question, I think that if a reasonable distance was set for how far each person rendered content and in what detail then having a area populated with 50-100 doesn't sound like it would be a big deal. The fact that the C4 Engine requires at least a GeForce 5200 is a plus so you don't even have to try to cater to people with worse- I mean the 5200 was considered an entry level card in 2003, expecting to settle with something more useless for gaming 6 years later would be crazy. 50-100 people over a large space is a large difference than 50-100 people in the same SPOT though, especially if you add in weather and other particle effects- even the big published games can make people wonder if maybe they should lower some of their graphic settings. As for the whole issue about the online game being chunked up and spread out- I think you would probably need a handful of dedicated Network Programmers.

Re: Question about networking

by jwatte » 06 Jan 2009, 18:03

caldoer,

With all due respect, it doesn't sound like you have actually shipped a massively multiplayer anything. I have (ever since 2001). If you do physical simulation (> 10 Hz step rate, server-side collisions, actor/actor interactions) then the limitation on server scalability will come from the physics. There's a reason that almost no FPS allows you to create instances with more than 64 players (some go to 128, and console ones typically go much lower than that because of the low CPU power on consoles).

C4 runs all the simulation on the servers, as well as on the clients, at least in the "default" set-up as used by the demo game. Additionally, it doesn't show players until after the authoritative data has arrived from the server, so the other players you see are not forward extrapolated. What someone called "lag compensation" is often one kind of forward extrapolation -- where you draw something "forward" in time, guessing about future state. Again, C4 does not do this.

Of course you can add whatever you want -- you have the source.
