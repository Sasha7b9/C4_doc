Re: Underwater effects

by Frank Skilton » 29 Dec 2007, 00:18



  • Reflection volume
    This controls the overall relative volume of the early reflections and reverberation. This is combined with factors determined by the size of the room and the reflection volumes of the sounds themselves. 100 is full volume, but the slider goes up to 200 so that reflections can be made artificially louder.
  • Reflection HF volume
    This controls an additional volume that is applied to high frequencies in reflections and reverberation. 100 means no difference between high and low frequencies, and 0 means that high frequencies are completely removed.
  • Reverb decay time (sec)
    This controls how long reverberation lasts in an environment.
  • Medium HF absorption
    This controls how much the high frequencies are absorbed by the medium (air, water). 0 means no absorption, and higher values mean that high frequencies are removed at larger distances.


Access these properties by selecting the acoustic space and hitting Ctrl-I to access its node info screen. Make sure reverberation effects are enabled within the sound options GUI, or by setting the following variable to true in the Variables.cfg file:

Code: Select all
$soundReverb = "1";

by Eric Lengyel » 21 Aug 2009, 03:20

a2k wrote:Actually, I take that back. I can get some reverb going. So maybe I'm misunderstanding how the absorption is supposed to work, because I don't notice any muffling occuring.


There needs to be a bit of distance between the sound and the listener to get the absorption effect. It's meant for outdoor areas.

a2k wrote:Also, I think I'm misunderstanding how the acoustic spaces are supposed to work at all, because when the camera (assuming that's the ear position as well) is outside of the acoustic space, it sounds as if the camera were in the acoustic space, so I don't notice a difference inside or outside of the space (but still in the same zone).


There's actually a lot going on here. It depends on where the sound sources are as well. If the source is inside an acoustics space, but the camera isn't, you still get the reverb, etc., for the distance travelled inside the zone with the acoustics space attached.
