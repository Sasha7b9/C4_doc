Re: Wingball

by Adam Golden » 16 Dec 2011, 08:46

This is a very important announcement for fans of this game: I just blew my night (and opportunity for sleep) to finally get my terrain LOD system functional, and wanted to share the excitement before I get into 'real work' for the rest of the day.

As most of you reading this know, the current version of Wingball (0.6) has no terrain LOD - it's all rendered at the same vertex density no matter the distance. My obstacle to finishing LOD before (although started/semi-functional) was not being able to avoid gaps/glitches at the seams between separate geometries which were not at the same LOD. I came up with a solution last night that works. Here's a quick overview of the difference. With 16 blocks visible (2 square km at the size and density used in both current maps):

LOD 0:
131072 faces (16 * 128 * ( 8 * 8 ))

LOD (max):
30720 faces (16 * 30 * ( 8 * 8 ))

With blocks of the higher-density (current) LOD 0 up against blocks at the max LOD possible (i.e. least triangles), there are no gaps. The loss is absorbed within the central area of each patch (8x8 patches per block). Vertices at the edge of each patch are maintained, hence the limit of this technique is determined by triangulating just those edge vertices, which for 9x9 as used in the current maps is 30 faces, vs. 128 right now. I doubt I'd go that low, I'd like to keep some central definition too - but it's working!

The result of this "breakthrough" (aside from short-term implications) is that I'll be able to contemplate streaming again. This was abandoned in favor of preloading a smaller map instead due to the large number of distant blocks paging, which was resulting in hiccups when turning around or moving quickly which only got worse with view distance, obviously. With 75% less data to page initially for those blocks, it now seems like a reasonable possibility again. That isn't to say I'm going to dump time into streaming right now.. the maps are big enough as-is and don't take long to load so I'm not prioritizing it.

I integrated/automated the LOD generation and switching last night, and tonight I got everything new functioning in the Optimized build, so that's pretty much good to go! A quick performance comparison on this laptop (Mobility Radeon HD 4250) shows roughly 25-30% frame rate increase with view distance maxed (a bit less than across 1 iteration of the map), foliage off for consistency and LOD1 starting at a distance I'm cool with. I'm using the view distance slider in performance options as a bias slider but I'll probably provide both. Might now be reasonable to create larger maps too.. but honestly, who wants a course longer than crystal sands "endurance run" - what was I thinking, I haven't made it to the end at all lately, but that could from trying with a touchpad instead of a mouse.. or maybe the course just sucks and needs a new layout

Tried the new water to replace my previous ocean, grabbed the material from hells mouth demo and tweaked from there. Couldn't figure out why it wouldn't work until I realized I hadn't placed reflection/refraction portals - after that no problem. The automatic horizons are great, I had a "topless box" underneath the water level before to simulate that. I'll spend some time customizing the shader a bit more and "warming up" the simulation / exporting wave data before the next release.

At the edge of view distance I'm planning to fog out the world (blend distant objects into a skybox haze) instead of popping stuff in/out or fading directly. I think it looks better that way - had it implemented before but couldn't get my own attempts at haze to distort properly at the horizon. I'm also going to unify view distance of terrain and objects/foliage - better to eliminate pop-in for good.

Re: Wingball

by Adam Golden » 23 Dec 2011, 20:49

Here's a comparison shot of Wingball terrain before and after putting some time into mesh simplification/LOD. Not only is it now appx. 1/3 the number of faces for the same view distance, the quality at the level shown below is nearly lossless - and I can regenerate specific LODs and with more or less aggressive simplification. I just reasoned it out through the difference between the z of a particular vertex and the average z of all vertices connected to it. The closer to 0 the value gets, the less noticable it is to remove. Exactly 0 means it probably won't be missed (shown in the pic). I'm sure it's not the best way to do this, but it works well enough to simplify a linear grid like this. The maps now preload both lod sets and it still takes less time than it did before. Anyway.. this wraps up my changes to Wingball terrain for now - It's not perfect, but it's better
