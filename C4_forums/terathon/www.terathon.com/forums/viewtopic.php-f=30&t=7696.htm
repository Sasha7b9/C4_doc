Re: New Divergence Video and Screenshots

by ethanc » 19 Jul 2009, 01:43

Argoon wrote:Impressive guys really good job. "thumbs up"

Two things that i notice and you guys certainly must know, is that the character moves is head and the suit neck armor also moves with it, seams unnatural, also the character seams to float above the ground, Is that a problem with the C4 physics? .


Yeah that's only in a few instances. The deal is that the video was shot at two separate times by two separate characters. In a few shots you can notice as you did that the collar moved with the head. This was already corrected as you can see from the closeups of the character inside the Northstar (about halfway through the video) but the character taking the first couple angles used the suit before it was corrected, thus the collar going with the neck.

But as I've stated, it's been corrected.

The second issue, the character slightly above the ground, that's because the options to clamp the characters feet to the ground weren't always ticked during animation importing the last time it was done for that model but it's just a matter of re-importing with the option checked. Not a big thing.

It's just such a pain to re-import the massive amount of animation data we have that we generally only do it when there's a major update involved rather than over and over for small things. It'll be corrected sooner or later but right now there are much bigger fish to fry.

by ethanc » 27 Jul 2009, 05:22

Fratercide wrote:Real impressive Ethanic, I want to give you some props for your props.......
{also kudos on the water}

Can you tell us what some of the challenges (and wins) you came accross while developing Divergence?

and of course whats next?


Crap, sorry I forgot to respond to this one man, but better late then never eh?

The water came out better than I anticipated actually, thanks for noticing
With the exception of the shoreline where it meets the surf of course but honestly, can you remember the last game you played where the shoreline looked decent? It's just one of those things developers have been bashing their heads against for years it seems. The last time I looked at a shoreline in an MMO and thought it was decent was actually AC2, which is now dead and gone.

If you ask one of our few programmers I'm sure they'll say something like, "The biggest challenge is getting middleware X to work with system Y".
If you ask an artist they'll say, "It's remembering to keep helper nodes and nubs out and remembering to close off all that geometry and unlearn what I was taught in school about culling faces.

Personally though, I would definitely say that the biggest challenge, and probably the one that breaks the overwhelming majority of indie games that might have good ideas, but don't pan out, is just people in general.

Making an indie game, and doubly so for an indie mmo, is basically a hopeless endeavor. To make it happen you basically need either tons of money, tons of time, or leadership who can still function for long periods of time in the face of almost certain hopelessness and when things go to shit, can they recover.

Things will go to shit over and over and over. Everything will come together, then it'll all fall apart, and then you have to put it back together wash, rinse, repeat.

The biggest struggle is keeping the team together, focused, and with as little downtime as possible, which means person X isn't waiting on person Y to add feature Z, and it's even harder now that we have no money. However after a few years, it's definitely good to have a few extremely devoted people who for the first time can actually see a payoff on the horizon. In a way, people (including myself) work even harder now because of the state of the game than when we were funded, although the pressure is intense.

What's next, other than the integration of 1.6 is getting things ready for a combative alpha. If it weren't for the whole "mmo thing" we would have had this a year and a half ago. There are C4 hobby games like Vortex of Hostility and Visible Dark that, as good as we look, blow our stuff away and are already past that stage, but once again the girth of the project comes from those three letters, MMO, which means you need things you dont need with a standard FPS. Huge things, expensive things. If we can get a combative alpha going, I think we can alleviate some of this pressure, bring in some additional funding, and buy ourselves hopefully another year to work on the more intricate aspects of the game, like the skill system and eventually crafting.

In a nutshell, we need to raise at least $200,000 in the next 3-5 months to continue what we're doing and for everyone to be doing well once again, so if in the next video you see people shooting each other in the face, consider that a good omen

Here are my top 3 favorite "wins".

1. The biggest "win" was probably seeing my face digitally captured and stuck on a characters body of my height and weight in a videogame that I designed. That was a huge affirmation. I'm hoping for even bigger ones in the future.

2. Being condescended by, and referred to as "a competitor" by a major developer at a convention.We are in no way a competitor, nor a threat, but the knowledge that at least someone thinks so was really something to behold.

3. Being asked to deliver a keynote speech on "success as an indie mmo developer" and having to say, "I really appreciate the opportunity, but we aren't successes... In fact, I can't even afford to go to your convention" to which I heard back, "Oh, my apologies. We just saw the things you had available and assumed" (paraphrased)
