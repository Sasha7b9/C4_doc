by datalol-jack » 21 Mar 2014, 10:44

I agree. Switching your main game engine in mid-flight is daft, and I wouldn't recommended it. It almost never works out the way you hope, and several good studios died because of this error. A playable, well-presented project that is stable on the intended hardware is worth more than a hot mess of bad PR, day1 patches, 30fps lock, and game-breaking bugs. I'm testing different options now because I can afford to and throwing away crud code isn't an issue; but if I were to start something bigger -- a full game project -- I would pick the most stable base possible. And stability is the chief attraction of C4.

For the other players, I think it will come down to: marketing, documentation, content, community. Ultimately that's what keeps people around and the sub fees flowing -- the MMO way. Yes, there are risks associated with a large number of mouths to feed, but in the middleware business it is far less than in AAA game development and new IP creation. Both contenders can throw good money at the pillars I've mentioned.

On the matter of tools specifically, I wouldn't knock them now, Davaris, not at least until you've given their latest incarnations a go. They are really quite good even for one-man teams, and C4 can pick up a few paradigms from them in speeding up the design workflow and asset pipeline management. I'm having a whale of a time compared to what the quirks and clumsiness of Blender 2.35 and other legacy apps put me through back in their day.

However, in the long run it would not be wise to go head-to-head with these monoliths, and I'm sure Eric has been in the tech industry long enough to know this. So, if you can't beat them vertically where do you go? Sideways! There are many niches where C4 can shine; destructible voxel terrain, for example. I have faith in Eric's skills and experience to sufficiently differentiate C4 for a wide variety of games, and to do things correctly in terms of architecture of the whole project, which may be a less visible boon but a biggie regardless.

As for pricing plans, those can always be rebalanced to fit the new market conditions, as and when Terathon feels it is appropriate. It happened before.
