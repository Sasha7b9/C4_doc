Re: Game developed by solo dev, "Banished"

by datalol-jack » 01 Feb 2014, 11:52

Yeah, I tend to miss good betas like that. >.< Too much interesting stuff coming through greenlight and it is impossible to stay on top of it all sometimes!

That much experience certainly helps one to stay focused. Advanced skills don't hurt either! But as for infinite/streaming levels - they aren't so hard to do. It helps if you know how scrollable tile-fields work in 2D, though. I would suggest you start there.

The basic idea is: store your world geometry info in a string table/text file; create a memory buffer(some modern variations use VRAM) to stream to, as the camera advances unload level cells/geometry chunks farthest behind the player; stream a few lines ahead in the file, load the cells approaching the horizon into your buffer then construct them in the world at low LOD; repeat. In effect you are just navigating a table/file when you do this. For procedural generation there's the extra step of actually crafting the world at run-time and taking care of potential glitches that entails (huge variability of terrain, random water patches, etc), then saving the info continuously to your string table to avoid doing expensive calculations again.

Now since C4 uses voxel terrain and forward rendering, you would need to take care of light, portal placement and running the transvoxel algorithm if needed (just extra info to store really). Or you could go with terrain patches instead/heightmaps, but you would lose the ability to graft overhangs and other nice cavity features in your terrain. After that there's just testing to do to reduce pop-in, improve performance on low spec hardware and ensure nothing stalls/crashes.

There are two examples on YouTube done with C4:
http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_1t5YadLvx8
http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V95jo-Tnado

I believe both members are still active on the forums, so you could ask them about their particular approach, if you are curious. I'm sure they won't mind sharing code. The technique has been around since the late 80s (I think, in terms of research at least), so it's definitely doable in C4! Just look at early big world games: Elder Scrolls, Sim City, Populous, Powermonger, Magic Carpet, GTA, etc. There's also a channel of a guy making a physics based builder in the sky! From what I gathered he already found ways of using C4 methods to make voxel terrain on the go.

Although there are no inbuilt methods for level streaming atm, correct me if I'm wrong, the roadmap does talk about run-time terrain modification / destruction, which would make things even more interesting and easier to do on the fly (traditional approach is to use heightmaps and chunk those, but voxels work perfectly fine with some modifications to the approach).

Anyhow, that's my 2c for the day.

I've been reading through his blog and just got done reading the AI and pathfinding entries. It's nice to know that AI and pathfinding are notorious systems to get right when developing, and that they need a lot of iteration to get the best possible performance. It was interesting to learn that he used a simple state machine for his AI. I believe Davaris came to the same conclusion, that Behavior Trees were just not worth the time and effort to use for a one-man show, and he went back to state machines. Now that I've gotten my Behavior Tree system at a good point, I wonder if I'll eventually turn back to state machines as well. Authoring BTs, while straightforward and flexible, have a significant amount of overhead to just get components authored. I really wonder if I'm just damaging my iteration time for the long run.
