by jwatte » 23 Jun 2010, 00:45

Are impostors generated as a pre-process?

It'll be interesting to compare the C4 impostors. Especially if you support flight or other elevation differences -- it sounds like the cruciform will be visible from above? Or does "45 degrees" really mean more like a 1-4-8-4-1 sphere of rendered images?

Another common approach for (dynamic) impostors is to render any object or set of objects smaller than X or further away than Y into a set of render targets, from the current camera's point of view, and substitute those render targets with a quad instead of the geometry. If you use larger render targets and slice them up, you get to draw many impostor quads in a single batch.

Because those impostors are rendered at runtime, that means they already cast good shadows on themselves.

For good shadows on the surroundings, you simply support one set of impostor projection parameters per camera, where a depth-shadow-light counts as a "camera".

Impostors using this dynamic mechanism reduce the render load, becasue you remember the camera position/angle used when generating the impostor, and calculate the deviation error as the camera move, and only re-render the "worst" X% of visible impostors each frame, meaning you get an easy 10x improvement in vertex performance. Fill rate is still about the same, because the objects cover the same amount of screen space, but that's not generally a problem, because impostors are generally used for things that are further away so they don't fill the screen.

Imposter generation falls into two categories. Static or offline imposters are those that are generated offline, usually by an artist. Statically-generated imposters have been in use for many years and are more commonly known as sprites, particles or billboards. The classic example in this case is the often seen "billboard tree" constructed from billboards at right-angles. This tree is an example of an imposter that has four viewpoints. This article is about dynamically-generated imposters. They are similar in concept to static imposters and are still implemented as billboards; however they are regenerated at runtime by rendering an image of a 3D object to a texture.
