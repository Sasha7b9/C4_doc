Eric, please confirm that the following scenario would be legit, as I see a lot of self-funded indie teams wanting to follow something like this if it's allowed:


- A team where initially a total of N C4 licensees are needed (ie total of N programmers that are writing C++ code that extends C4).

- One of the team members has the engine source, and is making mods to it, that all programmers (including himself) are using that build of the engine.

- Teammates own 1 Standard License under one license number.

- Teammates own a total of N - 1 Beginners Licenses under different number(s) - necessary b/c you can't comingle Beginner and Standard

- all team members funnel their questions through the 1 Standard Licensee to the private forums (funneled, not directly shared accts!)

- a person leaves the team, later another individual joins

- a *new* Beginners License is purchased for the person who joined, becuase he/she can't use the other (ie non-transferrable)

- all licensees are kept up to date (ie annual upgrades payments are made; nothing lapses)

- the game launches commercially, having at that point used a total of 1 Standard and N Beginner licenses.

- revenue starts to flow in

- the first N * X dollars of *team* revenue is used to upgrade the Beginner licenses to Standard
(where X = current upgrade price)

- after that (ie Terathon has been made whole), disbursements can be made as appropriate to all team members


Do you see a problem with any of that?

Now for a slight variation of the above...

What about the artists, designers, etc. who aren't C4 licensees? Or even a programmer who brought their own fully Standard license to the table from the beginning? Do they have to wait after Terathon is made whole before they can see any revenue distribution? That is a subtle difference, but could have a substantial impact to certain members of a team.

In other words, is Terathon to be made whole of all licenses that need ugprading before *any* team members see revenue, including those members that weren't C4 licensees? This may be a problem for some teams that set proportional sized percentages for team members based on their contribution. For example, and artist who had nothing to do with C4 and had by far the most amount of total effort into a project may not be pleased with the idea of all of the programmers getting $350 worth of compensation (remember...they take their C4 seats with them) before he sees a penny.

Given that team members own C4 licenses and not teams, I think most teams are going to want to structure the compensation rules with independent buckets per member, and what goes into a specific programmers bucket initially is what pays off his license (or upgrade), at his personal rate of revenue sharing. Within the team this will certainly seem fair to all, but it does mean it takes Terathon longer to be made whole.

For example, there could be one guy who was on the team for a week who had a Beginners license and did make a tiny contribution, and he's drawing at 0.25%. The game will have to sell $100,000 before Terathon would be made whole on that license!!! If the game in total only sells $10,000, then Terathon won't have been made whole on all license upgrades. However, that one programmer never would have seen a penny either, because his split was only $25; not enough to cover the "upgrade". So maybe that's fine.

This may sound like splitting hairs, but this minor point has a big impact on not only the team, but Terathon as well.

Re: Basic Edition to be available for preorder

by Eric Lengyel » 22 Jun 2010, 17:07

OK, I think there are two pieces of information I can give you to clear this up:

1) The Standard Edition and Basic Edition are not interoperable. So one team member using the Standard Edition could not provide engine binaries to other team members using the Basic Edition. The Basic Edition users would not be able to build the game against the standard engine. Thus, any serious team needs to be made up entirely of Standard Edition users.

2) The Basic Edition prohibits the act of selling a product made with C4. You have to upgrade to the Standard Edition before you even attempt to make a sale, not when you've sold enough to pay for the upgrade. A commercial product cannot contain binaries built with the Basic Edition.

In general, the Basic Edition is intended for individual non-commercial use. For anything other usage scenario, the Standard Edition should be licensed.
