by MachineryFrank » 16 Jul 2010, 01:25

I dont know of any problems. Maybe it is just some slight material configuration. I dont know. I remember that all vegetation looked good so far in C4. Sometimes I have to change ambient color and ambient light. This hides the fact a bit that foliage is actually made out of planes. The reason is that some planes can get much more light than other ones. But this does not always look naturally. So one can reduce the effect of light a bit on it.

I saw that another indie engine does not light grass at all and uses some baked ambient occlusion data for tree foliage. It is not a bad idea. But when I have plants with very big leaves then I prefer real lighting without such fakes. It really depends on the kind of models you use.

When I watch at games made with SpeedTree then I realized that foliage always turns the planes towards the player. So you see it always faced to your camera while moving. This also means it can always look into another direction. But the lighting does not change much. They probably also use a quite global lighting model. When all foliage looks into the same direction then there cannot be much of lighting difference.

Ethan, I have read this thread again and I have to check this tutorial shader material out. But again: Even when this shader provides "correct" lighting from both sides it will not look ideal as I described above. It will reveal the planes of the foliage much more in many cases. You will see planes of grass more easily when one side is bright and another dark.

I am not sure how but I think there needs to be another lighting solution to have ideal results. From my experience with still rendering it often helps to play with ambient values to hide the lighting a bit. Lighting is good on the root and the thick branches but it can be bad on transparent planes faking foliage.
And it also helps to have lighting from all directions, like some global illumination. I also have good results with a dome light. So the idea to have faked ambient occlusion on vegetation (like another engine is doing) is not a bad idea.

by MachineryFrank » 25 Jul 2010, 15:13

Ethan. I checked one of the models of your pack. Recalculating vertex normals in C4 gives the same result. So I think there is no problem with normals. Here are my results:

no lighting:



vertex ambient occlusion:



standard lighting:



C4 tutorial shader where I replaced the textures for diffuse, colour variation, spec and normals:




In the end I can only repeat what I already wrote: Realistic lighting reveals the planes and makes the result often worse instead of improving it. I personally prefer the ambient occlusion. If we could combine AO with some simple lighting on foliage and realistic lighting on the bark/roots then it would look much better. Otherwise it could be done like SpeedTree does it. They just rotate the planes towards the view camera. So there is no lighting difference on all the foliage. They also use some AO as far as I know. The inner foliage is a bit darker compared to the outer leaves.

This also makes a lot of sense. In a real outdoor world there is not only one light, the entire environment lights a tree. Especially the sky lights a tree. So the ambient occlusion part is very import. When there is an overcast day then the global illumination in form of AO is the only light source.
But when we light it with only one light source then it will always look artificial.

Here is another exsample of the same tree in a still render software:

one directional light (sun):



one directional light plus a dome light (half of a sphere like the sky):



one directional light plus global illumination using some background colours as additional light source:


The image with only one light source looks worse. I personally like the last one most.

So how could we fake it? We need some ambient occlusion data to fake global illumination. The vertices in the middle of the foliage should store darker ambient light. The sun should have not much influence on the leaves but more on the bark/stem.
