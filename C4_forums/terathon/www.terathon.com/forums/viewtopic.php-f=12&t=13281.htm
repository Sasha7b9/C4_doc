Stereo Rendering?

by gogo » 30 Oct 2013, 23:23

Hi,

It's been a long while since I've posted, but after searching the forums, I couldn't find an answer for this.

There has been a lot of talk in the Virtual Reality research community about finding an engine that we could all use (well, most of us anyway). There are so many positive points about C4 that make it attractive for VR, so I advocated for it. The main questions I got were about support for stereo rendering, and rendering within immersive CAVE environments (think about a large (3 meter/side) cube you could stand in, with all the walls, floor, and ceiling stereo back projected). Typically, each wall is rendered by a separate machine, and they are all synchronized.

Is there any hope for:
1) Stereo rendering that would drive an Oculus Rift, and/or
2) Multi-machine synchronization.

Thoughts?

Re: Stereo Rendering?

by Eric Lengyel » 30 Oct 2013, 23:57

Nice to see you post again.

We actually just ordered an Oculus Rift devkit, and we're waiting for it to show up. It's a virtual certainty (no pun intended) that C4 will support stereo rendering very soon.

CAVE style rendering has also interested me for a long time (and I've envisioned cylindrical enclosures as well due to my experience with dynamic panorama rendering in the 90s). Multi-machine synchronization for this would probably be somewhat more difficult to develop, but it's something I'd eventually like to do. It's probably not going to happen in the next year unless we get significant funding specifically earmarked for it.

by gogo » 31 Oct 2013, 22:50

stanlogan wrote:My son is bugging the daylights out of me to get one of the rift dev kits. This means that I eventually will have to cave and get him something I've wanted for a while .

I have a Pixy on the way (http://www.kickstarter.com/projects/254 ... ion-sensor), and have a vague plan to use it to develop an alternative to this project: http://www.kickstarter.com/projects/623 ... -made-real

Having this kind of support in C4 will bring me that much closer to being able to do it. I hope the dev kit arrives soon, and the development proceeds quickly and effortlessly .


I also have a Pixy on the way, and it looks very cool. The Atlas is really nothing new, and very easy to implement with things like ARTookKit/NyarToolkit. The whole idea of being able to "walk in the real world" in VR is also not new, and does not solve the problem of mapping a finite real world to an infinite virtual world. Redirected Walking is one way to do this, and has been shown to be very effective. There are also many others, such as walking in place. Still, people drift A LOT without the visual cues of the real world to help, so doing this stuff in the Oculus is very hard. People are going to bump into everything.

The main problem with the camera-based techniques for tracking movement is the size of the camera. Yes, you can track your torso, but what about your weapon? What about your hands? Other kickstarters are also looking at this, but multiple kinects is still the way I would go for cheap, large-area tracking of the body.

It's nice to see all this new VR tech, but the VR research community has done a lot of science showing how doing actual work (and play ) for long periods of time is hard regardless of the tech you use. So much fun to work in this field!

Now we need a good stereo game engine to drive our work further!

dbouvier wrote:In regards to Z800 how is the head tracking? That is one thing I was really impressed with on the Occulus, the only better tracking I have found is TrackIR and only because newer versions support 6DoF so you can move your head closer which is great for looking at cockpit instruments closely and really completes the whole immersion.


The head tracker on the z800 is terrible, so we don't use it. We use either the SpacePoint fusion tracker, or the high-end 8-camera PhaseSpace system we have.

dbouvier wrote:I'm really looking forward to seeing C4 support the Occulus because we do plan to support Occulus from the start as it fits with our designs and it seems to me that they'll produce it for a cost a decent number of gamers will be able to afford and being able to say C4 supports it too can only be a good thing imho.


Me too. Most VR researchers use a very nice standard abstraction layer between peripherals and applications (games) that allows us to quickly switch between tracking systems without even recompiling. It is called Virtual Reality Peripheral Network (VRPN) and is very low latency. It has many nice features too numerous to list here. Have a look: http://www.cs.unc.edu/Research/vrpn/
