by Jesse Meyer » 19 Jun 2012, 12:47

Hey Phil.

To start, welcome to C4's community forums!

As for the frame rate hitches, it would be helpful for us to know your computer specs and OS. Also ensure your DirectX run-time is up-to-day as well as your graphics drivers, or all your drivers for good measure. I run a dual core Core2Duo with a Nvidia 460SE, 8gig RAM, so fairly average specs, and I don't experience serious frame rate hitches. I do notice momentary pauses when entering a new area, and this is due to C4's shader compiler -> Gfx driver preparing all the new necessary shaders for first use. Once they've been generated, I THINK they don't have to be generated again, but that's a bit of a moot point since most users play a scene once. I don't think there is a robust way around this issue, because the drivers are very very slow at this, for some reason.

For me, C4's overall performance is very good, and it only gets better with each update (there is a new part-2 visibility system in the works peeking at the roadmap). It really depends on a lot of factors so it's hard to give you something concrete, but from what you're aiming to work with, I think C4 would be a great fit.

Really impressive games - You mean AAA quality games? I'm not aware of any, and I know a lot of projects are currently under wraps, but the ones that are public can be found here : http://www.terathon.com/c4engine/shots.php

Re: Framerate drops in demo scene

by Eric Lengyel » 19 Jun 2012, 18:27

There are actually a couple places in the Hell's Mouth level where the rendering load gets pretty heavy. It's due to the fact that the water shader is causing a reflection scene and refraction scene to both be rendered in addition to the main scene. It's really a level design issue and not a problem with the engine performance in general. We're looking into the exact cause and will make some changes to eliminate these.

You should definitely see better frame rates by switching V-sync off. You should also be able to set the V-sync to "smart" which will dynamically disable V-sync if the frame rate drops below 60. (This is a new feature of the Nvidia drivers.) This will prevent the frame rate from dropping all the way to 30 if you really could be rendering 59 fps.

One more thing, if your GPU is capable of SLI, then turning it off (in the control panel) can boost your frame rate. There have been problems with Nvidia drivers unnecessarily copying a lot of information from one GPU to the other, and it slows everything down instead of making the scene render faster.

Nidvia Optimus

by sammy » 25 Jun 2012, 14:59

For future reference:
For those of you using laptop with Nidvia Optimus (pc automatically switch graphics mode to nidvia or default intel), even if you install the newest graphics driver, directx runtime dist. or v++ runtime, you still have lag with C4 engine, try the following:

You guys need to click on your battery icon on the bottom right of your task bar and select "more power options." Select "high performance" as your active one then click "change plan settings" and then "advanced power settings"

From here you'll need to change EVERYTHING you can find to the highest performance possible. Most should be default at highest since you're on high performance mode but keep an eye out for anything that could lower performance (power saving states and stuff).

Now go to your NVidia control panel and select "manage 3D settings." Under the "Global Settings" tab select NVidia as your default GPU. Scroll down and find "power management mode" and select "maximum performance."

That should do it.

http://forums.nvidia.com/index.php?showtopic=212110

The fps increase a lot!
