Re: Loops not looping well

by Eric Lengyel » 14 Aug 2013, 00:27

Thanks. The file you sent contains 0.026 seconds of silence at the beginning, followed by a short fade-in. If that whole file was looping seamlessly in another program, then that other program is broken.

Generally speaking, the sound mixer in C4 has been around for a long time and has been thoroughly battle tested. Looping and concatenation, in particular, are guaranteed to be sample-exact and perfectly seamless. The engine does not start and stop multiple sound buffers from the perspective of any lower-lever library, so it's impossible to have a break of any kind at a loop point. As far as the audio driver is concerned, C4 is just constantly streaming one sound buffer forever, even if no sounds are playing.

Re: Loops not looping well

by Eric Lengyel » 14 Aug 2013, 03:46

C4 ignores any markers, cues, loop points, acid chunks, etc., and always loops the entire sound. To date, we haven't really had a compelling reason to support loop points .wav files, but it may be something we recognize as we transition to a proprietary format. In the particular case of the file you sent, the best thing to do would be to trim the file to the loopable data and use the engine's fade in capabilities when it's first played to avoid a pop.

Re: Loops not looping well

by Eric Lengyel » 14 Aug 2013, 13:37

I don't necessarily mean to use the scripting system, but you could. If the sound is something that is triggered by a script, then you could set its initial volume to zero and then fade it in using the Vary Source Volume script method. In direct code, the Source class has a VaryVolume() function that you can use after playing the sound.

The engine also has the ability to smoothly vary frequency, and that's what's being used on the proton cannon in the Lasers level. It always loops the whole sound. If the sound were instead divided into a power-up, main loop, and power-down, then there would be no way to smoothly transition to the power-down part because you could currently be playing anywhere in the middle of the looping part. The gun can also power down from any point inside the power-up part, so it would have to somehow find the exact spot in the power-down part that matches the power-up frequency -- not something that could be done with loop points. It's much better to just vary the frequency instead.
