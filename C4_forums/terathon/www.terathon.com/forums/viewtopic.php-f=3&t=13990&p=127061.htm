by Rastar » 04 Dec 2014, 16:11

Welcome!

Yes, for that kind of money the C4 engine is a steal (it actually already was before...) Is it indie-friendly? That depends on your expectations here. There are engines where you more easily can click together some simple demo, making you believe that it's easy to "write your own game in 21 days". C4 won't do that to you. You will have to get coding in C++ right from the start to get your own game going. In return you'll get an engine where you're much less likely to hit a major obstacle. For one, because you have full source access. Second, because C4 is neatly designed and very optimized. And thirdly, you'll rarely get your questions answered by an engine's maker with a comparable dedication and similarly detailed explanations.

by Eric Lengyel » 05 Dec 2014, 05:19

sabino wrote:-Physically Based Shading


This is supported, but you would need to create the specific techniques you want to use in the shader editor. We're planning to add pre-built shader blocks for common techniques in the near future. (Cook-Torrance reflection is already available as a pre-built block.)

sabino wrote:-Save,Load/Pause,Resume.


This is all built into the engine.

sabino wrote:-FPS suitable


FPS is a strong point of the engine.

sabino wrote:-Multiple worlds (a lot of arenas to play in)


No problem.

sabino wrote:-Physic - Fracture
-Physic - Fluid
-Physic - Explosion
-Physic - Deformation


You would need to be more specific about what you need with each of these. C4 support fluid surface simulation with real waves and buoyancy, and it supports rope and cloth based deformation. Objects can be pre-fractured like many of the items in the demo. You can also see explosions in the demo that create force fields and delayed chain reactions. I suspect you're looking for deformations that permanently modify a solid object, and that's not directly supported at this time, but I'd bet it could be done with a little cleverness.
