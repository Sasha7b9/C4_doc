MIDI music?

by David Vasquez » 11 Jul 2006, 01:15

Players tend to play the types of game titles I am developing for months on end, so any WAV-based music will get pretty old since I could only fit so much of it on a CD. MIDI files are amazingly compact compared to WAV files, so I could fit a hundred times more music in the same amount of space, assuming I can find that much music to license.

Can anyone recommend a good third-party MIDI playback library that has a decent sounding set of instrument samples? The last time I checked, the General MIDI stuff that came with Windows for free sounded like a cheap Casio keyboard at a flea market, so I'm hoping to do better than that.

Also, does anyone know of some musicians that have MIDI files for which they have full rights and which they can license for use in a gaming title at a reasonable price?

I'm not sure if it makes sense to request MIDI playback as a feature in C4 Engine, since this feature is a bit esoteric.

Thanks.

David Vasquez

by seryu » 11 Jul 2006, 01:27

The only way midi music is going to sound better is with a better soundcard. The soundcards included with motherboard are crap for midi.

You could use adpcm to compress wav files and play them in C4, or add support for vorbis ogg.

If you want to include midi files + midi instrument library in your game, you are going to need a lot of MB.

Timidity converts midi to wav in order to do what you want (and its a way to play midi in linux):
http://sca.uwaterloo.ca/www.cgs.fi/tt/timidity/

by mikec » 11 Jul 2006, 12:48

I agree, the only real benefit of OGG is for streaming music without the MP3 licensing hassles. I don't see why there couldn't be a separate thread that handles the streaming updates and decompression. For short one-off sound fx though, there isn't much point to using OGG at runtime since you'll have to decompress it anyway. However, it would be interesting to consider using OGG as a packaging format that gets decompressed at installation time -- so as to keep the package footprint smaller if that sort of things matters to your game.

Okay. The audio layer in Doom3 was fixed by the same guy that did the audio engine in Unreal (Carlo Vogelsang). What they did was shifting the expense of sound processing onto the sound hardware via DirectSound (a new and special version of OpenAL was provided through Creative as well as new releases for all of their modern sound card drivers). This resulted in a 25% FPS increase(!). Imagine the massive supports id software gets. IIRC Eric does not plan to use OpenAL.

EDIT: I rechecked. Apparently hw in this case is beeing used as a cache of unpacked ogg samples. So the trick with ogg decoding is predecoding all samples in storing this in RAM. Storing on hw and combining that with hw mixing adds up to the fps increase.
