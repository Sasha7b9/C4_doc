1. Since C4 can understand Voxels, and therefore volumes of space, is it fair to assume that your implementation of a Physics Engine will function at both Local and Relativistic scales?

2. If so, would it be possible for you to implement Tachyons so that players can see forward through time to the actions they will be taking in the near future?

3. Also, what about Tardyons? These could also be handy since it might be possible to use relativistic physics to thus compensate for lag...

4. Will I need to design my larger levels (such as the galactic core) using Riemannian geometry, or will there be a tool to translate them to n-dimensional space provided?

5. Will this new physics engine allow me to build a large nuclear accelerated gun that fires a bullet at 0.99999 C, causing all objects within the game to be suddenly and irrevocably drawn into a small pinprick, from which no further rays can escape? 9. In this final eventuality, with whom would the rights to the physics engine exist considering the new universe would all be running from my desktop?

Re: Physics video of the day

by Nilithius » 08 Sep 2009, 02:57

wil wrote:1. Since C4 can understand Voxels, and therefore volumes of space, is it fair to assume that your implementation of a Physics Engine will function at both Local and Relativistic scales?


Voxels have no effect on the 4th dimension.

wil wrote:2. If so, would it be possible for you to implement Tachyons so that players can see forward through time to the actions they will be taking in the near future?


There is questionable evidence as to whether or not tachyons actually exist, and even if they did, there's little chance we could use them to see into the future.

wil wrote:3. Also, what about Tardyons? These could also be handy since it might be possible to use relativistic physics to thus compensate for lag...


Everything that is not a tachyon is a tardyon. No reason to capitalize them, by the way.

wil wrote:4. Will I need to design my larger levels (such as the galactic core) using Riemannian geometry, or will there be a tool to translate them to n-dimensional space provided?


Depends on the density parameter you plan to give your universe. Keep in mind that if local geometry has constant curvature, global geometry will be heavily constrained.

wil wrote:5. Will this new physics engine allow me to build a large nuclear accelerated gun that fires a bullet at 0.99999 C, causing all objects within the game to be suddenly and irrevocably drawn into a small pinprick, from which no further rays can escape? If so, have you decided on a position on the matter output of the black hole, will my game be eventually consumed by it?


C4's not a ray-tracer.

wil wrote:6. Will you be implementing wave/particle duality and/or a system for the generation of quantum fluctuations? This would be handy in as much as it would allow people to see the black holes I wish to place around my level before they fall into them, seeing the visible radiation fallout on the event horizon.


Player characters would have to be exceptionally large not to notice the accretion disc surrounding the black hole.

wil wrote:7. How do stencilled shadows and/or per-vertex lighting behave when in the presence of an extreme gravitational force, such as a worm hole or similar sub-space anomoly? Will the shape be warped in real time or will you be using a lorentz transmorgrification based on a voxel reading of the gravitational flux within the area?


The proper name is "Lorentz transformation", and - assuming all light would be trapped in the black hole - there would be no shadows, and no reason to perform the readings in the first place.

wil wrote:8. Will I be able to implement my own Wolfram-style universal form code to create a big bang and then re-simulate the universe within the physics engine? If so, have you accounted for the possibility of the strange and, i admit, rather unlikely situation in which a direct copy of the universe leads to the Game re-creating the C4 engine within the parameters of it's own physics simulation, thereby causing a paradox that may potentially shut down our existing universe, and all life contained within?


Wolfram's ideas are flawed in that they require an unknown source of calculations being performed continuously in the background in order to create the very particles his universal model denies existence of.

wil wrote:9. In this final eventuality, with whom would the rights to the physics engine exist considering the new universe would all be running from my desktop?


As long as your not charging in excess of $100 - or have a publisher who is a member of the ESA or ELSPA - you have full rights.

wil wrote:10. Will this mean bigger Gibs?


Not in the demo, but I'm sure with your knowledge, you can program your own.
