Re: Simple Quad Lighting

by Adam Golden » 07 May 2012, 23:28

CptnWaffles wrote:"Does anyone know of any current solutions to this?"

If there's no easier way (not sure without digging, which I can't atm), you could write a custom controller and try things like SetQuadColor or SetQuadAlpha depending on the result you're after, based on [at least] distance from the nearest light source. You'd most certainly want to store pointers to those light nodes - and positions if they don't move - then traverse that instead of the scene graph when updating. You might also consider updating only after a given number of milliseconds have passed.. if a lot of nodes get their shaders invalidated every frame, you will notice a performance hit.

Re: Simple Quad Lighting

by Eric Lengyel » 08 May 2012, 23:05

Jesse Meyer wrote:Does anyone know the official reason why particles are not affected by lights sources?


Particle systems can be affected by light sources. Several particle effects in the 2.8 demo use this capability. The subclass's Preprocess() function needs to make the following call before the base class's Preprocess() function is called:

Code: Select all
SetEffectListIndex(kEffectListLight);


See the BloodParticleSystem class in MGEffects.cpp for an example.

Eric Lengyel wrote:
Adam Golden wrote:
CptnWaffles wrote:I didn't think setting quad coloring required the invalidation of shaders.

don't know that it does.. haven't looked into it


It doesn't.

Thanks - good to know.

The point of my response was, if you have 1000 quads visible in your scene and you're setting colors on them all (which is conceivable given the purpose of this discussion), there's going to be a noticable difference between distributing those updates across many frames (best, smallest hit per frame) vs. reducing the frequently of updates to somewhere that the max velocity of the character would never make them noticable (best, if there's few enough to do all at once without causing any stagger) vs. an arbitrary update in every Move (bad, unless you will never have more than a few being processed at once).

I benchmarked something similar which did require invalidating shaders, and given the results of that I might just be over-cautious now of over-processing. There's a lot of room for optimization, keeping that in mind. Even when it looks like you need something every frame, in many cases there's just specific events that should force an update right away - but cycle at low priority otherwise.

Edit: In this case just turning on effect lighting would be the best solution lol - my answer was to provide direction towards "any solution" if that wasn't possible.
