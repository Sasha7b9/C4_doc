Re: Version 1.5 Final Now Available

by jwatte » 19 Jan 2009, 20:38

The problem is that C4 actually *needs* those more advanced features. If the card can't do it, C4 simply can't render.

Now, pretty much all game developers who do it professionally these days realize that the PC FPS market, where you can say "screw Intel and 64-bit chips," is small and shrinking. The two big markets for entertainment software are mass-market entertainment (could be casual, could be "interactive movies" like Nancy Drew, etc), and console games, where the platform is 100% known. The only genre where the PC shines is the MMO games, and that's mainly because the patching, UI and server set-up is easier to deal with on PC than on consoles.

In fact, I believe the makers of high-end hardware are quite depressed, because newer MMOs are actually targeting lower spec machines, because their money making potential is squarely proportional to addressable market size (not only first-generation players, but the friends those players will draw in).

There's significant risk that C4 on PC will be mostly a technology demo and a "foot in the door" technology, and the real applications will be the Xbox and PS/3 versions. Requiring high-end graphics hardware on PC these days means just severely limiting your potential market, and developing content to take advantage of that high-end hardware actually costs a lot more -- a double whammy!

Re: Version 1.5 Final Now Available

by Harry Tuttle » 19 Jan 2009, 22:43

You can laugh all you want, but after much examination and experimenting, I have decided to shelve my C4 license for good and I am now studying the Sauerbraten source code. This engine with the addition of Newton physics and some decent AI is actually better for an independent developer than C4.

I would compare any well done map in Sauerbraten with shaders and fx to anything C4 has to show for itself and you'd find that Sauerbraten runs faster on older hardware and looks much better. I've created experimental maps of mind boggling size and detail without ever worrying much about textures or materials (LOD is all generated automatically in the background) and Sauerbraten not only looks superior, it plays at extremely good frame rates even on very old machines with no need to turn off everything but the polygons. The pipeline for assets alone in C4 is so convoluted and so confusing that it is nigh insurmountable for anybody who does not own Maya or 3D studio - even then it requires much patience and is slow as molasses. I don't know how many years old C4 is now and still creating a character with bones in it ala MD3/MD5 takes a month of work and two months to fix. That's just not realistic for indie game authors to expect them to spend that much work on assets alone.

Agreed. There's engines out there that are much better for rapid development than C4. The flip side of this is that if you want to do anything beyond rapid development then you will come across limitations. Each engine has it's own limitations and it's how they fit into your project. Saurbraten/Cube2 has it's own limitations. Of the open source engines you can't go far wrong with Delta3D (<-- my opinion only and not to be taken as fact).
