Since the GUI discussion has popped up again in the lounge, I am posting here instead:

Good work flow can be hard to measure but things like:
Total Number of Clicks / Mouse Motions, Key Presses, and total time etc to achieve the goal are a good start.

An "Icon with optional Text" is very useful. Tooltips in general are inferior to the presence of Text + Icons until the user becomes familiar.
That is why I love it when I see a GUI has options for ICON+Text, ICON Only, Text Only, and of course Tool Tip Options.

It turns out that a "skin" and color scheme does make a big difference and this improves Work Flow. Here is an example:

I was working in an "Older" Autodesk product, where the work flow was familiar and it has a good setup for Icons and "pages" on the left, top, and right.
BUT, the COLOR choices for the UI were poor... making it difficult to see all that I needed to see (Grid + hidden lines contrast was poor). So, having the ability to "easily" change color options is an important feature of a GUI.

Another example was when MS changed the UI for VS, it drove me crazy until I could return it to something that felt natural.

I should point out... that "Usability" is not the same as having "The Ability to Modify"
The color settings for the C4 Engine are modifiable... but it also needs to be "user friendly":
That means: The menu options for changing the colors should be IN the world editor with instant visible feedback for each change perhaps in an example template.

Most important: The GUI should NOT be a single party solution by majority opinion.

What I mean is this:

Let's say 60% of the people prefer Tabbed Interface Design with Fixed Left and Right Columns and Icons on the Top.
And 30% of the people prefer dockable pages that can be moved anywhere with Icons along the top.
and 10% are miscellaneous.

The point is... if you only go with the majority you are disappointing 40% of your customers.

Flexibility and the ability to move GUI elements in user customizable layouts... will improve the total % of satisfaction.

by Meow » 23 Mar 2014, 11:19

We were using different definitions for aesthetic it seems, I meant it more in the noun sense. For me it is similar to beautiful code. Beautiful code is aesthetically pleasing but it also follows a aesthetic similarly to how a Cubeism or Art Nouveau follows an aesthetic. A good code/UI/workflow aesthetic is at a minimum clean, efficient, easily understandable or simply it does exactly what you expect it to do in the simplest and fastest way possible. There are rules that govern it but it also becomes almost effortless to move along the flow. You get there through hard work, logical reasoning and a ton of testing/data but great code/UI/workflow has something extra, feeling/sense.

A example could be shooting a gun. You can and should learn to shoot in a technical/logical way, but if you look at many great shooters they shoot by feeling/sensing, they have very little need for sights. For them shooting a gun is no different than pointing at someone with your finger or moving the mouse pointer to the exact location you want even though you have multiple monitors, its hard to describe logically how you do this because its a feeling. In my experience the difference between good and great usually has a lot to do with how it feels, which is why I said interface design(and workflow design) are a full-time job, it requires a lot of work to get it right.

ok. Only the end-user will be able to specifically determine what they like best. Anything else is a best guess by the initial interface design.
Aesthetically pleasing is uniquely personal and individual and very task specific even if it is well liked by many in its base configuration. That is why I advocate for a highly customizable GUI.

Ultimately, I think we are desiring the same thing. However, I am specifying how I want to get there.
There is the "Nebulous" -- I want an aesthetically pleasing GUI that flows well.
VS
The Specific: -- I want a GUI that does not dumb down my options, and is highly customizable, something that I can save/load my layouts for specific tasks.

Its very very hard for Eric to "define" and create "aesthetically pleasing" because its so highly individual. Where as, highly customizable...WILL BE Aesthetically pleasing, because the end-user can control how it looks.

Eric can make a highly customizable GUI, even if he does not know how each person wants their GUI to be customized individually.
The end-result of that is it becomes Aesthetically pleasing. without having to define it in advance by a specific layout.

I agree that it can be personal and that is where customization comes in, or even a UI that intelligently adapts to your style of working. ZBrush may be an ok example, C4 and it are similar interface wise but Zbrush's interface offers a lot of customization. Implementing that level of customization is not a simple thing. Leaving everything up to the end user is an impossible way to design, good designs have limits imposed for a reason..having too many ways to do the same thing or even seemingly similar things cause unneeded confusion. That's one of the reasons people get scared off from C++ and end up using something like Ruby/Python/Clojure and I say that with all the love in the world for C++.

Mostly I'm fine with C4's interface, I think it could use some unification in some areas for example the connect tool seems redundant and should probably be handled by a script. C4 would possibly benefit from a dyanamic window manager, meaning a mix between tiling and pop out windows. that way information like a node's info box show up automatically, it would also allow you to place widgets anywhere you want and based on your use they would have preferred locations where they show up if they become a viable option in your workflow. Another option would be to have a ZUI, this is a 3D application after all. there is no reason we must stick to a purely 2d UI paradigm when we have access to depth. C4 has the ability to do things you wouldn't be able to do with a native windows UI. A UI that functions extremely well when using something like Oculus Rift, head mounted displays are the present and future after all. All I'm saying is you shouldn't limit your self to thinking in terms of...Maya, Visual Studio and other horrid designs.
