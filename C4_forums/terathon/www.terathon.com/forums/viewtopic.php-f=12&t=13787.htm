C4 on Linux...

by Wolfsong73 » 23 Jul 2014, 23:19

So, decided to do a system reinstall and, for a change, I decided to go with Linux instead of Windows. I learned of a dist. called LinuxLite, which seemed more up my alley (lightweight, stable, etc). So far it's been awesome. Just about everything I've been using on Windows is open source and/or supported on Linux anyway, so there was little keeping me there. Even Steam's game library is expanding impressively. About half the games I own on Steam are already supported natively on Linux, whereas last time I checked it out, only maybe a handful of them were. They run great. So, definitely progress there. For anything that still don't work well on Linux, there's either WINE, or VirtualBox... though I doubt it'll come to that.

Now, knowing that C4 will compile and run on Linux, it was one of the first things I installed. Compiling was super easy. I just went with the command line option, rather than installing the IDE, and it went by without a hitch. It baffles me that more engine devs don't offer a similar option for those whom, like myself, just want to "compile and go", instead of requiring the whole setup process with an IDE, etc. Nice touch, Eric. Kudos.

The real joy, however, was when I launched the program. No joke, barely a second later, the logo movie was playing. Opening a map takes no more than 2-3 seconds, and the performance, for the most part, is smoooooth. Far faster than my Windows 7 install. Sexy. I can't find any means of seeing the FPS while in-game, but I'm sure I'm pushing near 60fps.

I am not on a "beefy" system by any stretch. You can't even buy some of the components I have in my system anymore (mobo, CPU, etc). Still, it runs awesome, at all high settings no less.

I did have a bit of a bout with some screen tear, despite having Vsync enabled. It persisted both with C4's vsync options, and with NVidia's settings. Some googling quickly turned up similar issues in some Steam games, and it was mentioned that having "Compositor" active could make it happen. So, I disabled Compositor and, voila... no more screen-tear.

There's also an occasional "hitch" when a map first loads up and I'm turning the character/camera in different directions. It's not "severe", but it's noticeable. The effect reminds me of what would happen in some Unreal Engine 2 games, when it was loading stuff on the fly as you moved around.. but not as bad. It does it maybe a few times, an then smooths out again.

Anyways! Just wanted to share my experience with C4 in Linux so far. I'm going to dig back into learning it tomorrow, when I *should*, in theory, have the Beginner's Guide in my hands.

Cheers!

Re: C4 on Linux...

by Eric Lengyel » 24 Jul 2014, 00:05

Glad to hear it's working well.

Wolfsong73 wrote:I can't find any means of seeing the FPS while in-game, but I'm sure I'm pushing near 60fps.


Type "time" in the console to bring up a little window that shows overall FPS and some GPU timings.

Wolfsong73 wrote:There's also an occasional "hitch" when a map first loads up and I'm turning the character/camera in different directions. It's not "severe", but it's noticeable. The effect reminds me of what would happen in some Unreal Engine 2 games, when it was loading stuff on the fly as you moved around.. but not as bad. It does it maybe a few times, an then smooths out again.


This is due to new shaders being compiled as new combinations of materials, lighting types, and fog environments are rendered for the first time. The Nvidia compiler is particularly slow compared to other OpenGL implementations. A standard IR format for OpenGL would go a long way in eliminating these hesitations, but such a thing unfortunately doesn't exist. Once each shader has been generated, they get cached in binary form, so that's why things smooth out after you've been playing for a little while. I'm planning to add some stuff to the engine that will cause at least a good portion of these shaders to be compiled ahead of time when a world is loaded, and that should reduce the frequency of the hitches considerably.

Chrunchbang is my current distro of choice, it is Debian based but it uses Open Box and is insanely fast which is great for software development, when I'm coding things like Ubuntu's Unity or Compiz just get in the way and slow things down.

It's great when you want to use someone's computer but you don't want to worry about if their computer is secure(which it never is..).
