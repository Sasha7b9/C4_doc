So now that I have a license I've been playing around with the engine. I can say that I'm in love with it. I wish I could get more people to see how awesome it is. Keep up the awesome work Eric. Oh and all this testing I'm doing is on Linux. A great advantage to have since it seems Windows 8 has upset many gamers and Linux is in high demand on Kickstarter.

Re: C4 Engine, a dream come true

by migizi » 31 Dec 2012, 21:53

Having games to give exposure to an engine is great but I think there are other issues. From my experience developers are getting lazy and want the fast easy way. My experience is Unity3D offers that. You can rapidly create a game in Unity and they have gotten a lot of exposure through Kickstarter. There is also the fact that some schools are using it because you develop so fast. This is the direction my school took. It was a great tool to get started but you start to see the limitations the more you use it. You either deal because you don't care or find something better.

I've already discussed where I want to take the engine with a buddy of mine. My plan is to write the code using simple models and then get funding for models and textures. If I can get the exposure on a site like Kickstarter I think it would help everyone. People need to see that games don't need more hardware to look amazing, they need better tools.

by Robert » 02 Jan 2013, 06:52

Yeah, it seems kinda hard to get Artwork that works well with C4, and all the converting really freaks me out sometimes. However, no other engine can compete with C4's strengths, but that only appears once you started using it.
Once C4 has more versatile importers (or, lots of exporters in content creation programs like blender into the own standard Eric was mentioning) or own types of asset libraries that can be easily purchased, imported and work, it might become a real option for many people who're scared by a too complicated workflow that often produces strange behaviours because you didn't know the one or other trick.
"Some" engines generate a lot more income through their easy content management / store system than through the engine itself. An interesting approach, however, i do find that the quality of the content in such installations is often quite low.

Which makes me think. Would quickly available, good quality content improve C4's stand on the market, and if, how would that be possible ? I do bet that the own format idea Eric mentioned would be a great step forward.
