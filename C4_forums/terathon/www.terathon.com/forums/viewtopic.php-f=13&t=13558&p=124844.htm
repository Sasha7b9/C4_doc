Re: Real-Time Path Tracing

by Jesse Meyer » 14 Mar 2014, 11:26

Animation is a somewhat problematic for path tracers do to their statistically based lighting model. Still scenes on average require a steady number of samples per pixel to achieve visual equilibrium, but when segments of the scene are in motion, the sample size fluctuates non-linearly which creates a difficult computationally juggling problem.

Certainly good active research topic.

I haven't a clue what it would take to displace the current shader based industry standard. It's fun to see this kind of model being possible today in realtime but I have doubts that it'll ever become a commercial alternative to what's in use today.

by datalol-jack » 15 Mar 2014, 06:48

Would be interesting to see what they are up to. Much brute-force computation is certainly going on, whatever the method. Heck, most interesting problems in computing hardly ever yield to an elegant analytical solution. I suspect the hardware is finally getting there for early birds to pillage a few research papers and start a few ventures. Isn't the normal lag between academia and industry something like 20-40 years? By that approximation the future should be here in about 5-6 years tops. Parallelism is certainly a virtue of RT. I don't think traditional renderers can compete in the long run on pure realism; especially if Moore's Law fails in the near future, and the metal starts improving laterally rather than vertically. Whether it matters to people is another discussion all in itself. The art and tech skills associated with classical pipelines won't go away overnight.

I do hope they are wheeling out some kind of sane, declarative material framework that behaves consistently across lighting scenarios; as in physically correct albeit an approximated way.
