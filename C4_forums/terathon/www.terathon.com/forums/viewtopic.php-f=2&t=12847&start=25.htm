Well there is nothing wrong with expressing your views ever, its not really hating... but I think its worth while to point a few things out in your post...

First and most important it's not a proprietary format, its an open format. So it will be possible for exporters to be created for any and all other model software tools, but Terathon has to draw the line at some point. They are choosing to work on exporters for the 3 most actively used tools being used by the community, but there is no reason in the future more could not be created by development teams, or you couldn't contract with Terathon specifically to have yours added. For sake of addressing something that is a real problem for some Licensed customers of this engine this needed to be done in a timely fashion with a focused scope.

Keep in mind that C4 will continue to support use of the Collada format, so for those tools things simply remain as they are now. You use that format.

Blender is free, and while it would be nice to have exporters made for any other free tools available would take significantly more time which is money for Terathon, and I did not see any Licensee's ask for support for any other tools then the three currently being developed for.

The bonus for the 3D modelling community as a whole is you get a new _open_ 3D exchange format that is well designed, implemented and maintained by a person/company who has a long history of providing terrific support.

That's just my take on it atleast...

As someone who has been bitten by issues with FBX export to import issues in the past between tools I am not surprised that it wasn't chosen, especially given its proprietary nature and the fact it has been known to be a moving target at times. Based on internal decisions made by Autodesk for Autodesk, which is great for them... but not ideal for other developers.

There's no format that can guarantee full support between 2 different applications. It's simply not possible.
Lets imagine a few situations. A new format is developed and it comes out.
Situation 1:
Format is very strict and guarantees identical display of simple models on every package that is officially supported. Why simple? Because every single modelling package has special features that are either fully or partially incompatible with other packages. So if there's something advanced it will be stripped. In this case the only benefit will be the fact that the models will for sure look the same in C4. Other uses are pretty much dead end and in this case the format is nothing else but another ActorX but with open documentation.
Situation 2:
Format is strict and the developer makes sure maximum number of features of all packages are supported in addition to full support of all base features needed for C4. In this case, there's pretty much no difference from FBX. It does the same thing. What's the point? It just makes harder for people to support additional packages.
Situation 3:
Format is well documented but has no restrictions on what can or can not be used. All features of modelling packages can be saved and transferred. In this case the format will become collada #2. Still, what's the point?

I can't imagine any situation in which everyone will benefit from another graphics file format. Is my imagination that bad?
