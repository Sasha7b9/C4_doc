by gautam » 23 Mar 2014, 07:28

I actually had done some business analysis and got to the point that the professionals who haven't ever seen the business side of things need to at least fail in 3-4 games before they can get to being able to successfully market their game.

This is how it had gone :-

Game 1 : Learn the lesson to make a game collectively. Here you will end up learning production values and stuff which you never had to take care like budgeting etc etc. Ofcourse marketing etc will come but focus will have to been on making a game. Here you mostly end up learning how to control feature creep. This is the major lesson one needs to learn first. Most money gets spent here.

Many of us unfortunately are bad at money and time management when it involves a lot of other people.

Game 2 : After finishing game 1 in time, you will have to realize the quality. You will have to focus on quality side of things. Quality is not about how good or bad or the next best something is but more about balance and to a sense of satisfaction. Part 2 of feature creep but with a better understanding of how to handle tight situations. The more games you keep comparing your games to the more feature creep will creep in.

Game 3 : Here is where you will learn the marketing. Marketing a game is all about sales. It is not about finding out how to make games the people want but more about how to make people want the games you make.

Game 4 : Utilize all you learnt in Game 1, 2, 3. This is where you should be in a position to make break even or profit.

I agree this may seem too idealistic to a businessman but there is no short cut to success but hard work, smart work and perseverance.

Most of the time though what I see is most people run out of steam or money on Game 2. And mostly my experience has been it is due to feature creep or wanting the next generation technology which was probably not necessary.
