by MACK » 09 May 2012, 21:45

Some video rendering features cannot be performed on the older cards.
So maybe dropping some compatibility is a good idea, when maintaining such compatibility will result in crippling development times to maintain backwards compatibility and also add new features.
As long as a decent amount of the user base can afford or are willing to upgrade, it seems a valid choice.

In fact, a lot of developers seem to make the mistake that they should program to the lowest common denominator, and while its a good idea to have backward compatibility, its a big issue for the indie developer who simply has no time and no budget to maintain "all" of that backward capability.

Eventually, a cut-off has to be made. With the price of DX11 video cards being so cheap... ie there's a TON of DX11 cards out there at a cost anywhere from ~$30 to $400, with a full range of speed and improvements, there every reason to require a minimum hardware requirement.
IF maintaining dx9 is truly holding back Eric, and Terathon's future development then the better choice seems to be:
go ahead and make the requirement. For those who are "unwilling" to require that their "clients" buy a new card, there is nothing saying they have to use the newer version of C4 Engine. Simply stick with the older compatible version and take a loss on future improvements.
So, yes some folks with portables will take a hit. And if this is concerning to the developer, then maybe sticking with C4 2.9 (or other) is the best option for that dev.

One thing we know is that a good game will drive a video card purchase. I also know that if I had a DX9 video card, and C4 2.12 came out with streaming terrain and required a DX11 card... I would not waste any time looking for a new card.
Most people will buy what they can afford, and most people can afford to drop $40 bucks even for a cheap card.

For years now there is a cadre of people playing FPS games who want to use a modem. Its sort of ridiculous to limit a whole group of high bandwidth players to the lowest possible... at some point someone has to drop the 2400 to 36K baud group and require broadband. (Or, spend a lot of time and effort catering to the lowest denominator).

Of course, Grognard is right... its important not to make the same mistake that Firefox made... and pump out new versions of software that don't offer backward compatibility with things like plugins every 3 months and no way to upgrade.

Fortunately, I have no worries about C4 adding in features. While it might be somewhat distressing to upgrade my code from 2.5.5 to 2.7 to 2.8 to 2.9, and soon 2.10... I EAGERLY sacrifice this time in exchange for the awesome features that Eric and Terathon have been adding to the C4 Engine.
And if it was not worth the time, then we can always skip upgrades until the moment is right.

I make this point often - you can craft a slick mostly-ajax site that works perfectly for the majority, or a progressively-enhanced site that works well and looks mostly the same for everyone. A great site that works perfectly for everyone AND looks great, doesn't have pages refreshing unless necessary and so on is going to be very expensive and limiting overall to create from a skeleton then enhance. It almost never happens. Bloggers preaching "the right way to do things" will NOT do it that way in the real world without deep pockets/open budgets and/or plenty of free time. The client just wants it done by tomorrow and doesn't care about archaic browsers or systems that will delay things a week and take designs to the cheap even if they can afford it. No time for philosophy - get it done! You can make your budget higher to support IE6, and you'll either get the extra money in your pocket for the trouble or walk away without it (and either is a positive result). Eric hasn't increased the price of C4, in fact he's reduced it - so something has to give eventually. I think if he can't justify the extra time necessary to support his IE6 with upcoming features, he shouldn't have to absorb that work on his own time either.
